3 Struggles with focus:
Starting (procrastinating). Why do we procrastinate? There is an aversion for this activity, or a craving for something else.Recognize when you are procrastinating. Acknowledge it. Are you more averse, or simply more interested in something else?Most of what is unpleasant in a task is an impulse that doesn't exist after the first few minutes. Take 5 or 10 minutes and just start.Try the pomodoro technique if 5 minutes doesn't help.I can only quit right after I have done something successful.Go back to the start to your ignored tasks and practice the pomodoro technique.You must be conscious in your time decisions.You cannot control your impulses, but you can learn to respond better
Sustaining (getting distracted). When you fail to stay focused.Flow, is often used as the model of focus to sustain. Flow is inconsistent with demand for deliberate practice. A flow state is not impossible in ultralearning, but it is unlikely.Resistance to what is natural is part of difficult learning. Don't worry about flow or seek it. Push through frustrating sessions. Your deliberate practice now will make later sessions more enjoyable.People remember more when practice is broken in different studying periods, and interleaved topics. Study various aspects/skills of a subject during your study session. Cover a few topics rather than one. 15 mins - 1 hour is generally fine for a subject.Sources of distraction:Environment.Task. Some activities are more difficult than others.Your mind. Random thoughts, restlessness, daydreaming, emotional wounds. Acknowledge the feeling, and gently go back to task. This will become easier and easier over time as you practice.
Optimizing quality (the right kind of focus).Two variables:Arousal: overall level of energy or alertness. Too much or too little is not helpful.High arousal: narrow, brittle focus. Athletics.Low/relaxed arousal: math, creativity. Wide focus. Eureka moments. Taking a break can encourage this by allowing mind to broaden view.

Directness - go straight ahead:He who can go to the fountain does not go to the water jar
Directly learning the thing we want seems too uncomfortable, boring, or frustrating. So we settle for some book, lecture, or app.
Directness = kind of like Transfer Appropriate Processing in psych literature.
Learning done with connection the context in which the skills learned will eventually be used.
Many self-directed learners fall into the trap of indirect learning. Duolingo is an example.Most students use listening to a lecture as the main way they learn material.You must do the thing you want to get good at. Spend a lot of time doing the thing. Do the thing and you shall have the power.
Sometimes the exact situation is not available for you to practice in.


Transfer: education's dirty secret.It has largely failed to occur. Learning something in one setting, and applying it in another.It's an education scandal.
A student who has studied examples, often cannot solve problems that deviate from the examples.
Transfer does exist, yet it is paradoxical. When we want it, it doesn't happen. But we do it all the time. Comparison is transfer. The more knowledge we get, it becomes easier to transfer.Most formal learning is indirect.Far transfer is reduced with greater direct learning.Directness also helps in that real situations share details with other real situations, as opposed to theoretical situations.
Learning subjects often sticks stubbornly to the situations they are learned in.
When learning new things, tie them as closely as possible to the situations you will use it in.
Just practice the real thing, orCreate an artificial project or environment.In abstract subjects, the place you want to apply these skills is less obvious.
Gaining a deeper knowledge of a subject will make it more flexible for future Transfer. The more you master, the more you Transfer. (The more languages you know, the easier to learn a new one)
Strategies direct learners use:Project-based learning.Immersive learning. Surround yourself with the target environment in which the skill is used. (Language immersion, joining skill communities)Flight simulator method. What matters for Transfer is the cognitive features. Simulating an environment will work if the simulation matches the environment.Overkill approach. Increase the challenge so that the skill level required is wholly contained within the goal that is set. (Speak at middle-schools instead of toastmasters). Put yourself in an environment where the demands are extremely high so you don't miss important lessons or feedback. This may feel quite intense. These fears are only temporary. If you start, you can often do it longterm. Aim for a particular test, performance, or challenge that will be above the skill level you strictly require. (Publish your work for scrutiny)

Drill - attack your weakest point:Like piano - take care of the bars, and the piece will take care of itself.
Your weakest points are like bottlenecks, limiting the speed of your progression.
Drills: simplify a skill enough so that you can focus your cognitive resources on a specific aspect.Drills take apart the direct skill into parts.
Drills vs direct learning.Direct then drill - method:Direct - learn language by speakingAnalyze and isolate componentsDevelop Drills and practice components separatelyIntegrate what you learn in direct practice again.
Earlier in skill: more switching between Drills and direct practiceLater toward mastery: more drilling, as you likely have lots of understanding of the skill and lots of direct practice done or built into schedule.
Designing a drill for improvement:Can be difficult.Timeslicing - isolate a slice in time of the longer sequence in action. Like a bar in a symphony. Layup in a basketball game.Cognitive components - Grammer vs vocabulary.Copycat - when practice of aspects is difficult to do in Isolation. Copy parts of the skill you don't want to practice from someone else, and only drill the parts you do need to practice. (Like getting a movie scene for practicing film scoring)Drawing other drawings. Editing work you have done in the past.Magnifying glass method - spend more time on one component of the skill than you would otherwise. Spend longer on specific components.Prerequisite chaining - start with skill you don't have all the subskills for. Learn as you go. Go back during the process to subskills to learn in greater detail.

Retrieval - test to learn:
It is better to wait and recollect than look at the book again.
Learning strategies:ReviewingTestingConcept mapping
Self-testing is what works best.Passive review has no feedback. Concept mapping is similar.
Judgements of learning - jol. If tasks feel like a struggle, we feel we have not learned them. Of they feel easy, we feel we have learned.
Whether you are ready or not, retrieval is helpful.
Free recall > cued recall.Cued recall > multiple choice.Delayed testing > immediate testing
Difficulty is part of the reason retrieval works.
Greater difficulty is more efficient.Things recalled are remembered more deeply when in difficult situations.
Testing is more efficient than review.Retrieval helps you prepare to learn better.Trying to find answers you don't know yet, helps reinforce search strategies.By confronting information you don't know, your mind reorganizes to find solutions.
Nobody has time to master everything.Some things are for mastery, some for recognition/application.
Knowledge that isn't in your head can't be used in direct practice.
Nearly any fact is online, but not having something in your head makes it more difficult to solve hard problems.
Great programmers know lots of ways to solve a problem, and can select the best one.

1. Do your research: what topic you will learn, and approximate scope (start narrow). Primary resources you will use. A benchmark for how others have learned this skill. Direct practice activities. Backup materials and drills. 2. Schedule your time. Decide in advance. Stay consistent if you can. Some tasks have long warmup times. Length of time for project. If you are unwilling to out time into your scheduling, you are likely unwilling to do the learning. 3. Execute. In some cases you won’t have the perfect execution. Can you improve it in Meta learning? Focus? Directness? Drill? Retrieval? Feedback? Retention? Intuition and deep understanding? Experimentation? These principles serve as directions not destinations. 4. Review your results. Not all projects are successful. The problems with projects can be often traced back to their inception, not our lack of willpower or ability. Successful projects are also worth analyzing. Hone and advance your learning process. 5. Maintain or master what you've learned. Maintenance (setting up a regular habit, integrate into life), relearning (generally easier than first time learning, subjects you need infrequently), mastery (initial projects offer new projects for subject followup). Not everything one learns needs to be done in a dramatic fashion. Example 1: low-intensity habits. Reading books. Day to day encounters. This works well for accumulation. Ultralearning is best for dealing with frustration barriers and psychological obstacles. Sometimes what is fun isn’t easy, and what’s easy isn’to effective. You can learn and grow to love what you learn, and at some point, your level of skill  makes the subject more fun to learn. Example 2: formal education. Train focu. Lets  Play and work blend seamlessly for children.  Teach people to learn on their own. Ultralearning is intrinsically driven, not extrinsically like formal education. You don’t need to feel that you are good at something to keep learning it, just that you could be good. It’some tempting to identify with labels of good or bad, and to compare ourselves with others. Many ultralearners chose vocations or areas of learning that were unordinary or abnormal, which made it harder for them to compare themselves to others. This is a learning approach, assigning people to projects they don’t know how to solve, just on the cusp of their learning or skill.  The more one learns, rhe more questions come up. Knowledge  increases ignorance, increased learning means both confidence and deep humility.  Our learning projects show us how much further we could have gone.  Our confidence is not arrogance, because we humbly know we have not learned yet, but we know we can learn.  Many things in life have a saturation point, but learning does not. Curiosity has a never ending life, the more we learn, the more we enjoy learning.
