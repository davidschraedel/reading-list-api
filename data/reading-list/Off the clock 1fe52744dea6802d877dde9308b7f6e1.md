Notes: People most satisfied with their time are intentional with their time, cut out unnecessary things, make plans, spend time present during leisure, know that good enough is good enough, and focus on people—knowing that people are worth the time. People who feel abundant with their time can feel off the clock during paid work. You can feel relaxed during plans you have made. Time is fluid, you are in control. People who do more meditative and reflective activity feel they have more time. Time is not to be feared. How to feel time abundant: Tend your garden, make life memorable, don’t fill time, linger, invest in your happiness, let it go, people are a good use of time. Leave time open on your calendar, and when needed activities must be rescheduled, you can move items to those times if needed. Track your time, be Mindful of your hours, and you will see the weeds and issues with the soil, etc. Mind your garden, and you will have more choices and time to decide. We tend to exaggerate the time we spend on activities. We think we spend more time than we do, and often spend more time on mindless activities than we think we do. Memory is not like a filing cabinet. The past is as much defined by how you interact with it now as it is by what happened. The richness of the relationship relies on the treasuring of the past. We feel like time is ours when we remember. More memories means more time. We feel like time is slipping by when we don’t remember what happened. Routines are comfortable because we don’t have to think about them, they are utilitarian. Your thousands or drives in the car can be consolidated into one drive in memory. Other parts of life are different. When we are young, all is new. Time passes the same, but when we get older, things seem familiar and monotonous, and thus time passes more quickly to us. The memory that stands out to us as adults is about novelty and unique experiences and change. Craft a mindset of adventure to create interest and memory in the most boring days. You don’t want every week or day to be long and novelty. Find a balance between routine and novelty. Intentionally plan time for adventure and new experiences. Three selves: anticipating, experiencing, and remembering selves. In planning and executing new experiences, we need to priorities the anticipating and remembering self. The experiencing self must be put in the background. The experiencing self has to endure tiredness or the pains of new experiences or using energy. Remind yourself: “all times pass”. The experiencing self is in the present, unsure of the good that will come from new experiences and effort, rather than liesure in the present all the time. Find ways to recall memories: ask people to tell stories, do something with your photos, journal, etc. Make some time to dwell in the past. Dwelling in the past requires compassiom and forgiveness for your past selves. Be a good steward of your time. But leave open space. Deckutter your calendar though it’s undoable, what if you only had things on your calendar that sparked joy? Let time flow like water, instead of having lots of meetings and space filled for no other reason than to fill space. If you make plans for things you like, it’s easier to say no to things people ask you to do that you don’t like. Declare a Jubilee for time, or amnesty where you will Marie Kondo  your calendar. Get rid of unintentional traditions. All things end. Be careful with the words yes to the future you’self commitments. Ask “would I do this tomorrow?” if not, maybe you won't do it in October. People won't notice most of the things you do. They are too busy in their own world, being busy for their own work or boss. Big changes in circumstances are often why we declare amnesties, though some people impressively decide on their own. When life changes, the things that are not crucial simply have to go out of necessity. Work that doesn't get you to the life you want is still wasted time. Create time dovidends: things that creates more time in the future, like hiring an apprentice or assistant, learning habits for your future self, teaching your kids to cook, designating spots for keys, phones, etc. If you will do something again, ask if there is anything you can do to make it easier or quicker the next time. Be disciplined, don’t interrupt your time with checking your phone and other things like emails. Be more mindful and present, and don't fill your time with easy scrolling or other entertainment. Do things in the real world that you love. Linger: the opposite of rushing, giving things the time they deserve. If you are enjoying something, stick with it. This can be part of your adventure, or creating new experiences. Tell yourself “there is nothing wrong”. If you have nothing else to do, just linger, lean-in, and be present. Enjoying the present isn't easy. Be where you need to be with enough time to relax and prepare. Late people tend to be wildly optimistic, and people pleasers. To avoid feeling frantic about time, stop being late. Observe how long things take. Just add in extra time if you can't estimate the time. For example, arrive 15 mins before the expected time. Savor the present, hold yourself right where you are. Take in your surroundings. Record the experience and savor it, with your mind and senses, creating more memory. Share the event with others, tell others about it in the moment, etc. Kill-joys: it could have been better, thinking about other places or times, etc. Cognitive distortions, and such. Appreciate the ordinary good in the moment.  Invest in your happiness: when people say they want more time, they often mean they want more time doing things they love, or memories. We want to spend time doing things that make us happy. Exercise, reflective activities, and interacting with people. Can money buy happiness? Happiness can be measured in many ways moment by moment happiness is often driving our moods. Money spent to reduce time on things you don't enjoy, and increase enjoyable time, is money well-spent. For most treats, fear of hedonism is misplaced. Invest in your happiness by making use of grown-up goodies (that pen that makes writing pleasurable, etc.) Pay yourself first, woth money, but also time. Look at your mornings. Do you invest time in reflection, exercise, praying, Journaling?  If you don't invest in yourself, time will fill that space. Time is elastic. If you do what you love, you still get other things done in the time alotted them. Not all life will be fun. Suffering is part of life, and can be meaningful if we change from it. We can also find moments of beauty and joy amid the suffering, and manage our time well to do things that bring us joy amid the suffering. This too shall pass. The mentality and perspective we choose makes a huge difference. Be grateful for the journey. Let go of unreasonable expectations. You can't envy, be perfectiknistic, etc. Be a satisficer, knowing that “the best” does not exist. Good enough is good enough. Love is finding someone who is willing to settle for you. Everyone marries the wrong person and then commits to make it work. We simply choose people who are compatible and of good character, and then make it work. Don’t be a maximizer, stop worrying. Just pick something thar a friend mentioned or somewhere you’ve chosen before. Pick a simple choice, and then have a simple alternative. Waste less time on maximizing, and then have more time for yourself and people you love. Focus on process goals (inputs, and habit goals, rather than outcomes). Small things done repeatedly, add up. Low expectations but consistency. Love is acceptance. People must be taken as they are. Let go of expectations for people. Making time for people can increase your time. They help you feel less stressed, offer connections, and give you satisfaction in your life, making you more productive. During the busy years, we are tempted to put people last. Be intentional about people, instead of spending time on things like TV and games, etc. Treat relationships woth same intentionality as work. Each quarter should have a career goal, personal goal, and relational goal. If your calendar is looking bare, that can be a nudge to reach out to people. There are people you naturally see often during your week, and people you see rarely. Plan ahead for solo activities with people/family. You can wait for opportunities, or you can plan for them, because life rarely lines up opportunities. Time is all about intentionality. You rarely show up to work without a plan or intention. Do the same for your home life, family, and friends. A good leader asks people to do things only he would do, building loyalty. Build loyalty by spending relaxed time together. In-the-hall chats can be a vital part of the job. Professional success still stems from relationships. Going to happy hour one night is balanced, you have all the other nights to be at home. Listen to people. Everyone likes to feel heard. Give chitchat at the beginning of a meeting. Networking is nothing more than building authentic relationships with people you are interested in getting to know and spending time with. Reach out to someone every day. Make a list of 5 people every Friday, and then reach out during the week. Start with compliment, offer advice if appropriate, ask for ideas, connect, etc. Most people could spare more friend time in their life. Go big with events, they are better excuses for meeting up or connecting. Make standing dates for smaller groups who are local. This makes easy recurring appointments predictable and on the calendar. Meet up with friends on business trips, collaborate with peers who can work with you. Be selective about people. Not all relationships will last. Some people will never deepen you spirit. In any community, there are always newcomers and those willing to welcome, and those who are not. Visit old acquaintances and friends. The happiness of your memory does not hinge on the present. Accept the reality of pain, and love anyway. Think about how people add to your life. Reach out to people by sending thank you notes. Using time better is a process. Do one small thing better today. All time passes, but some moments transcend the ticking. We simply need to notice, and soon we see more and more.
