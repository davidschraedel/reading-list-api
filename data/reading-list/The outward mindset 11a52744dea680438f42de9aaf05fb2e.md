Notes: Change behavior and mindset, not just behavior. When we break free of our inward mindset, we are able to consider actions we were not capable of considering before. All improves as orgs adopt an outward mindset. They see beyond themselves and narrow self-interests. Our mindset determines behavior and outcome. An outward mindset means we see others as people, not objects. Change is dramatic when we change how we see and think. What keeps people from an outward mindset is themselves. Your mindset is your choice. They may be ruining your life. Your bitterness is only ruining your life. I see people the way I see them because of me. We find justification by focusing on others’ faults. Shift your focus to the struggles/needs/burdens of others. When we refuse to see others’ needs, we seek to justify this mindset, and it comes ast a cost of the potential actions we could be taking toward the greater success of the organization as a whole. We often focus on ourselves during presentations and interviews, but the irony is that we perform worse. Focus more on the value you are providing to clients, what you can do for them. Outwardly “nice” performance is not fo used on others’ needs, but one’s own. We can convince ourselves that we are acting for others, but truly be serving ourselves. The cost of inward mindset is lots of wasted effort and ability. What times in your life did you feel most alive and engaged? When you focused on yourself, or others?  Do your work in a way that helps others do theirs. Inward mindset orgs do things. Outward mindset orgs help others to do things. The outward mindset pattern: 1. See needs/objectives/challenges of others, 2. Adjust their efforts to be more helpful to others, 3. Measure and hold themselves accountable for the impact of their work on others. SAM. They take responsibility for the full impact of their actions.  People usually hold themselves in higher esteem than than their company.  This often means they don’t feel responsible for the success or failure of the company.  As far as you are concerned, the problem is you.  Mindset shift start: Make the move you are waiting for the other to make. Widespread mindset change happens largely in response to those who change first. Being able to operate with an outward mindset when others do not is a critical skill. It is the most important move. people are often afraid that others will take advantage of them if they take the first move: this is an inward mindset. An outward mindset is fully aware and open to others. An inward mindset turns attention away from others and provokes resistance. Navy seals know they need to trust their fellow seals. Military might is built on comradery and outward thinking. Dipping a toe in rather than fully embracing a mindset change receives a lukewarm response. Others’ responses depend mostly on what they see in me. As far as I am concerned, the problem is me. The most important move is for me to make the most important move.people who think of direct behavioral changes often forget mindset, in favor of a quick fix. But how do the men committing crimes in the neighborhood feel not having work or food? That’s a long-term problem, that needs a mindset shift in policing. The quick fix is usually the slow approach to change if mindset is not addressed. At work: do I have a clear understanding of my manager’s objectives? Do my direct reports understand my objectives and how to help? In any organization, we are a central part of a collective result. Only with an outward mindset, leaders can position their people to be fully responsible for lack of success. Make everyone a thinker and doer, planner andimplementer, etc. People need all of their faculties, which are only exercised with the outward mindset (using the whole mind, heart, etc.). Controlling management inspires and encourages an inward mindset, and discourages the use of brains, particularly to help clients or customers. These abilities will attrophy and die with time. In helping people regrow this, don’t force your ideas and refrain from impeding their ideas.  People make assumptions about others’ abilities. They need a chance to thrive, and your whole outward support. What do you do when you need results, but you don’t want to create drudgery by telling people what to do? Help them learn about the objectives of their team, others’ teams, etc.  Help them start practicing the outward mindset.  Help them see how they are responsible to the success of the organization, and they will know how to start accomplishing the goals. Remove the barriers and walls of gatekeeping. Remove distinctions, the leader labels of status, because they don’t matter. What implicit cultural behaviors create distinctions and boundaries? How can we remove separation and make sure we get ideas and feedback from all levels of the organization? Don’t take yourself too seriously as a leader. Seeing others as people means we see them as people who can think, innovate, ask questions, problem-solve. A true review of performance would not pit employees against each other in a distribution. Tell your employees that their performance will be assessed based on their practicing and enacting the outward mindset. Those unwilling to adopt an outward mindset won’t successfully be able to imitate outward mindset outcomes and results and processes. It will seem risky to them to offer influence and power to those they see as objects. One person shifting to an outward mindset doesn’t force others to follow suit. Everyone still chooses their own mindset. But mindset is contagious. At times it is easy to have an outward mindset, because we feel cared for by others. At other times, we receive no return in kindness. Our outward mindset is an invitation, that sometimes is rejected. It is difficult to look outward when others do not, or when we have not done so for some time and are convinced we do.  Sometimes we self-justify when we feel mistreated, keeping us in an inward mindset.
