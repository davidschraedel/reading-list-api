Notes: Every building speaks, has a message. It speaks of longing for the past or visions of the future. Buildings talk to us, about the values we want to live by. Architecture is never neutral, simply beautiful, or simply functional, but always tells us something. If a form is attractive to us, it may because we interpret it as familial, as a mother’s thigh or a baby’s chin/arm, as human. Small visual changes can speak volumes about our psychological state, thus different arches suggest different feelings or characters. We sense in differences on human behavior a difference in feeling or interaction, such as contempt vs concern, etc. We can’t view objects without relating them to historical feelings or experiences or what we see their history. Our unconscious associative powers make these connections, though they are arbitrary much of the time. Many times, we dislike something simply because it reminds us of something else we disliked, etc. Genuinely beautiful objects may withstand our positive or negative projections and stand the test of time beyond our arbitrary associations. In a way, beauty is the promise of happiness. There are as many styles of beauty as there are happiness. The more we lose nature in our life, the more we seek it in our art, architecture, poetry, etc. The diversity of tastes in beauty is natural, as diversify of beauties is natural, and each serves a purpose for us. We need grandeur, but also coziness. If beauty could be made into laws, we would have created the most beautiful cities again out of raw material, easily. Social beliefs are like beliefs about beauty. We put names to characteristics, but irrationally come to these conclusions, borrow them from philosophers, or come to value them in haphazard ways. Yet there are some basic means we describe as beauty: order (surrender of individual to the greatness of the whole,  numbers, regularity/simplicity/predictability, coherence, consistency), order with complexity/intricacies . Contrast. elegance: There is beauty in durability and strength, paired with modesty.  Coherence:  buildings are choirs, not individual voices. The harmonize with their parts, and coherence with their settings (there is no Holland naturally in japan). Bad architecture is a failure in psychology and design. Create buildings that solve needs we didn’t know we had. Architecture lacking congeniality will arouse irritation, which people may not be able to describe, and may ascribe to mysticism beliefs or religious notions for lack of knowing how to express them. We can mold circumstances for the better, and the same with buildings done poorly. Our visual and emotional faculties need direction and guidance on what to focus on: we call it culture. Japanese have a different aesthetic taste in beauty, formed by their authors, artists, and philosophers. We often repress our preferences that do not fit culture, until someone is willing to hear it.
