Notes: Good overview of business storytelling and a basic story formula for different use cases. Stories that stick...
Stories are what make us human. We have the ability to create fictions individually and collectively.
Stories have a beginning, middle, and end...1. Normal life2. Explosion3. New normal
Not everything is a story:Stories include...• Identifiable character, or several single characters• Authentic emotion. The emotion felt by the characters or inherent in the story, not the audience. This is how to get empathy• A significant moment. Specific point in time/space/circumstance that sets it apart from the rest of our existence. Moment magnifies a specific piece of a sprawling space. Go small and detailed. Zoom in, be specific. Don't stay high-level or too vague• Specific details. Details and imagery to draw in the audience. Details signal to the audience how deeply the storyteller understands them. Specific details engage the imagination of the audience

Four stories:• Value story. Value to the people who need it. Value gap between product and customer.• Resist the temptation to fill gap with features and information. This is system 2 thinking. Lists, bullets, price comparisons, benefits, features.• Keep people in system 1 thinking. Automatic. Anything you can do to reduce cognitive strain is usually best. Ease, not mental strain• It's so much more than that... (the features)• If you can't talk about your product or show it to anyone, what would you say to customers?• - what's left? The people using it.• Value story• What pain or problem is your customer experiencing, what keeps them up at night? - normal• How does your product make their life better, solve the pain? - explosion• How is life different after, what pain points have vanished, how do they feel? - new normal• Have identifiable character. Have the person you offer services to be the center of your story, not your service. You must include a character to care about.• Authentic emotion. "You have to get into bed with them". What do they worry about before they go to sleep? What keeps them awake? The only emotions that matter are those in your potential customer, or the character in your story• A moment. Often connected to the explosion.• Specific details. You cannot fake thus. You need to know your audience, and some details they can connect with.• Founder Story. Every business has a story.• A founder story helps you stand out.• We tend to use the same differentiators as everyone else, thus we are the same. The solution is the founder story.• When all of the offers look the same, we go with the one where we love the story.• It's not the magnitude of the story, it's the decision to tell it• This story bridges the gap between the company and investors, building a team (securing talent), and getting customers• If you have started a business, you have a founder story• Where does it hide?• Go way back... moments in your young self• When you said "There has to be a better way..."• Look for the "blood, sweat, and tears"• Pitfalls• Founder story is not the value story• Not telling founder story because you are tired of telling it• This story isn't for you, it's for them.• Thinking you can't tell it, because you are not the founder.• Transition into the story. "I remember the day I heard the story, I was doing such and such, etc. The story as I heard it..."• "I knew..., and I hope you feel that too"• Letting reluctant founder shutdown the founder story.• If you tell the story right, authentically, it is not self-agrandizing• Stories sound like life• Identifiable characters: centered on founder• Authentic emotion: consider what audience cares about. Investors care you can handle frustration and doubt, and that you have determination and commitment, pride, etc. Customers care about what drove you to create the product.• A moment: include a big moment/explosion. A date, the weather, a day.• Specific details: audience specific. Path navigating• ... one of the easiest stories to forget to tell is the founder story.• Most of the time, you don't get a second chance to tell your story• Purpose story.• Purpose over profit. People are motivated by transcendent purpose.• We are wired to attribute meaning and purpose. If you don't do it, people will it it for you.• People use stories to align around goals or purposes. Uniting, clarifying, motivating.• Must be nonhypocritical• How strongly the story supports a specific message. What point do I want to make? What do I want my audience to think, feel, do?• Little room for error. Main character of a purpose story is typically the leader telling the story. If you tell it wrong, you will have told the story for the story's sake, becoming seen as an "arrogant" leader.• How to match: have a clear message. Then think, when have I learned this message? When have I seen this?• The best purpose stories are your own stories. Share the emotions you felt about the event. Vulnerability. Being vulnerability in business is the heartbeat of innovation, etc.• Running a successful business is vulnerability.• Purpose story often coincides with the explosion. Zoom in and share this part in slow motion.• Make the your own story feel likenit is about your audience, with specific details they can relate to.• Employees should know about their company history and story? "Family history". Brings meaning, purpose, and belonging.• Intentional storytelling is what builds and sustains culture. The same as it is for families telling their origin stories.• Do they know they are part of something bigger?• Customer Story. • Brand is what people say about you behind your back, when you're not listening• The customers tell the customer story. Not the company• The customer story thus has inherent credibility.• Ask for these stories.• Guide the response by asking the right questions.• What did you use before, have you noticed anything different after using it? Elicits a story with normal, explosion, new normal• Tell stories where there is a void in attention, when people have room for hearing a story.• Customer story is about customer. How to show them as relatable? Perfection is your enemy, there needs to be imperfect details to be realistic and believable, don't remove imperfection• Emotion from the normal, before the product. The new normal only matters in contrast to the old normal emotions.

Do I have a story? Everyone has a storyStory collecting:• Our stories attach to the nouns in our life.• People• Places• Things• Events• Think about firsts• First time you tried...• First date...• First...• May not be the story you choose, but will unlock more memories• Make list of customer objections and questions• Look for stories that resolve these concerns• Ask yourself lots of questions• When have you had to be resourceful to survive• What was the worst day in history of business• When have you made a customer cry for good or bad reason• When have you stopped a customer from crying• What's the hardest thing you have ever done in business• Who's life is different because of your business• What is your proudest moment in your work or business• What one event or decision in your history could your company not have survived without• When have you been surprised or mistaken about someone or something• What was your first or most meaningful sale• When have you lost a sale• Who is your most satisfied or least satisfied customer• When was your most embarrassing moment• When has someone said you couldn't accomplish something• What was the moment you knew your work is worth itStory choosing:• The right story vs any story• Pick from the four stories• Value story if you want more effective sales and marketing• Founder story if you want to increase competence and differentiate• Purpose story if you want to align and engage team• Customer story if you want better sales, marketing, and credibility• Which objective is most pressing?• About telling the story.• The audience• Who are you telling to?• What do you want them to think, feel, or do?
Finding stories in the moment:• Jot things down. Record what you see, the stories around you
..........Crafting your story:Ideas come and go. Stories stay
Explosions are usually where our stories start. This is when we notice the story.Start with the explosion and then work backward.
Normal: most fun and most important.Flex empathetic muscles. Help them settle in and let down their guard.Include details about character, emotion, particular moment, and familiar details for audience. This is a simple checklist to walk through
New normal:Use it as an opportunity to circle back to normal, but with the new information, product, benefit, etc.

Well played stories don't need gimmicks or tactics.
A story should be as long as it needs to be. Can be 10 seconds, or 90 mins.Start with the full-blown long story, write it all out. Then cut back on content and condense.Don't get rid of the most important parts for the sake of brevity.
Make your pitch a story, not a pitch. Elevator pitch is a story.

Craft the story for the goal of the story.You likely have the right story, but haven't crafted it well to suit your goal

Any moment can become a story.
Stories change moments.They change context, they change minds, they create connectionTell your stories

Telling your story.Tips:• Start with a story. Easy way to ease tension between audience and speaker• Be careful about images you use in slides. Let the audience create images from relevant context in their lives. They create it for themselves and thus find your story more relatable.• Only use nondescript images.• Don't rely on photos to tell your story.• Practice can break perfect. If you are too flawless, it doesn't work. A balance between being well prepared and over rehearsed. Let go of perfect, and it will let go of you.• Focus on the message• Tell the story that feels right to you. Only tell the stories you are comfortable telling. If you are not ready to tell it, or it doesn't feel right, don't tell it.• You don't have to overshare. There is likely another story to get the message across• When you have a real story to tell, it's natural. Storytelling is natural. Get out of your own way. If a story resonates with you, it will almost tell itself.
We want to hear stories, because we are human. Tell stories. Storytellers have influence.
Our lives are all story.
