Notes: Working harder is not always the answer. When you can’t try harder, it’s time to find a different path. Burnout is not a badge of honor. What if there are too many big rocks, they don’t fit in the container of time? Not everything has to be so hard. Essentialism is about doing the right things. Effortless is about doing them the right way. Making a free-throw is effortless action. Nothing but net, at peace, comfortable, fully here. Most important part of the process? Don’t overthink it. Get into flowstate. Computers slow when cluttered. When overwhelmed with information, our brain chooses to prioritize high priority emotions: fear, anger, etc. Removing burdens let's us see more clearly, be more energized, be in the moment. Our culture has defaulted to seeing important things as hard, and wrong things as easy. Hard = good, easy = bad. Challenge this thinking. We have a bias for path of least resistance. What if this essential project could be made easy? Puritanism embraced the hard and distrusted the easy. We seem to have inherited this view. Trying too hard makes it harder to achieve. Overachieving ≠ overworking. Effortless inversion: invert the question, what if this could be easy? What if all those “required steps” are not truly required? Try without trying. Those whonsucceed think that the essential tasks can be the most effortless. Anything can be effortless, but not everything. Find an indirect approach, simplify, a better way? Can you push something downhill, not uphill? Business strategy: Solve the easiest problem. Warren buffet strategy: look for no-brainer businesses to invest in, easy to run, long-term advantage, not difficult projects. “I look around for 1-foot bars that I can step over”. What’s the simplest way to achieve this result? Shed the assumption that the easier path has to be the inferior path. What if this could be fun? Essential work can be fun once we put aside the puritan notion that anything worth doing must entail backbreaking effort. Don’t separate all work from play. Lag indicators: you experience reward long after the action took place. Decrease the lag time by enjoying the activity, pairing the activity with the reward. Call people from your hot tub. How can you make it fun? Make the steps of the process bearable, or even fun. Don’t reward yourself after, reward during the process. Create building blocks of joy: like productive play with Legos, create order from chaos, make a task you dislike a ritual you look forward to. Rituals are about how you do something, like Marie Kondo’s ritual of letting things go when tidying. Rituals are habits with a soul, bringing us back to ourselves, a natural playful state. Release: the power of letting go. Do you have outdated goals living rent-free in your mind? They act like unnecessary background apps. Focus on what you are thankful for during your misfortune: a “have” state, a openness. Positive emotions creat upward spirals, open us to creativity and new ideas. Focus on what you have, to get what you lack. Negative emotions narrow our mindset. Set the positive emotions, gratitude, into motion, the system into motion. Eventually it will become effortless, as it gets easier and easier.  Rule: after you complain, state one thing you are thankful for. “What job have I hired this grudge to do?” Your grudge is not fulfilling the job request, hire another habit or activity. Accept people for who they are, that they are who they showed themselves to be, that you don’t need to expect them to change or do more than they offered before. Forgive and let go of their hurt. Rest: necessary. Dedicate mornings to essential work, break down that work into 3 sessions of no more than 90 mins each, take a short break (10-15 mins) between sessions to rest and recover. Do not do more today than you can completely recover from today. Same for a week. Rest is a responsibility. We get less sleep than people used to. Perception habits, attentional muscles. To be in the effortless state is to be aware, present, attentive. Turn toward: Receive the bid for attention and return the served tennis ball. Be present with people. Quakers’ clearness committee: focus person shares a dilemma met by silence, then committee asks a few clarifying questions, reflect or repeat back what they heard, but not give advice or opinions or judgment. They let people amplify their inner voice, letting them discover what is right. Prepare your space, rest your body, relax your mind, release your heart (forgive), breath in gratitude. The effortless state: what if this could be easy? What if this could be fun? What job have I hired this grudge to do? Sleep, recover,  and take a nap. Clear clutter, be present, ignore the irrelevant. More effort doesn’t always work. Pushing harder can decrease output. Wu wei: Effortless action. Define what “done” looks like. Stop at “done”. Swedish death cleaning: clean while you are alive, to avoid having your relatives do it when you are died. Define what “done” looks like for your life. The “now” we experience is only 2.5 seconds. That is where the change in our life happens. Take the first step in those 2.5 seconds. The simplest step is the one you don’t take, and it's easier to take no step. The courage to be rubbish. Everything starts out ugly, rubbish, or flawed, with mistakes. The faster you make mistakes, the faster you progress. Make failure as cheap/inexpensive as possible. Learn the easy way before the hard way. Let go of doing things perfectly. Have an error rate of 10-20%. Too perfect is imperfect. Writing is power. Even rubbish words are better than a blank page. Start small and begin making rubbish. A one-time impact is linear-action. Linear results are limited. Residual results are infinite in rewards. Perpetual results: effortless wealth. Use levers: learning, teaching, automating, trusting (hire, unify), preventing (strike problem at roots). These can also create bad results, but use them for effortless power. Learn: leverage the best of what others know. Easy fast solutions are tempting, but principles are the key. Seek principles. Principles are trunks and roots of trees. First principles are what you can keep, as there is too much detail in the world to memorize. The older a book is, the higher the likelihood it will survive into the future. Distill ideas into their essentials. Teach: harness others’ power. Teach people to teach. Simply the most important messages. Go for the straightforward message that can be easily understood. Automate: high tech = low effort. Anything that solves a problem with minimal human effort. Washing machines, thermostat, etc. What is a problem that irritates me frequently? What is the total cost of managing that over several years? What is the next step i can take immediately towards solving it? Hack at the roots. It’s never too early. Measure twice, cut once.  For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light.
