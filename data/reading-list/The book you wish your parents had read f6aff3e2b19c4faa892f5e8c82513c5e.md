Notes: High bursts of emotion at your child’s behavior is a signal that you are reacting to a past memory, to your experience as a child, not theirs. Think when this happens, “am I really reacting to this event in the present? Or something long ago?” look back at yourself when young with compassion. If you find yourself longing for a break from your children all the time, perhaps you need to give your younger self a break, more care, etc. Try revealing your inner critic: Write down your negative thoughts, do they remind you of anyone else? Drop “good” and “bad”. Stop judging and labeling. Describe what you see and appreciate, and praise effort. Praise what you want to see more of, comment on what you do right. Suspend judgment, and hold opinions loosely, as they it will all likely change. We make dirt for our kids, an environment, the soil, and let them thrive in it. Divorced parents need to have contact with each parent, and for their parents to communicate civilly. Admit rather than denying resentment if it exists. Then try to change. Life will change as you have a family, it will be difficult. Arguing: context, feelings. Styles of bad arguing: tennis attacks, squirrel distraction, becoming a martyr sacrifice to keep the peace. Ideally, work with one issue at a time and deal with what the issue is really about. The ideal argument is about understanding. Do it with I statements, and curiosity. Play the role of a mediator in family arguments. Think in terms of how each feels. Acknowledge feelings. Embrace your vulnerability. Don’t assume the intent of the other person. Is your relationship based on anxiety? Or is your partner your friend? Someone you feel calm with usually? Respond to your partner’s bids for connection/intimacy. Look for the good. Seek and ye shall find. Find things to appreciate. Turn towards bids, not away. Feelings: responsiveness to children/baby’s feelings teach them to interact with and hear their feelings, that they are not to be avoidedand will pass eventually.  Don’t disallow feelings, because you want your child to be happy. Don’t be in denial, that makes your child’s feelings more problematic because they are not letting them at as signals, and they learn to ignore their feelings, to stuff them down.  Encourage your child to draw, etc., how they feel. Help them be heard each day, to truly be seen.  When feelings are validated, they no longer need to prove their point. Talk about feelings rather than pushing them away. Feel with, don’t deal with. When children are felt with, they don’t need to feel it anymore. By validating the feelings, connections are made, empathy is practiced, soothing occurs.  By telling your child they are being silly, they won’t tell you anything remotely silly again. They can’t differentiate between issues and simple feelings. Closing off the channels of communication closes you off to warning signs, especially dangerous in terms of mental health, abuse, etc. That you would want to know about. The demand that we be happy undermines our lives.  We are often wrong about what will satisfy us. Turn down pain and sadness, and you turn down all feelings. Happiness is about relationships. Validating feelings strengthens bonding between parent and child. Distracting from emotions is a trick and manipulation. It’s a deceptive tactic and hurts trust and relationships. Help your kids with frustration rather than distracting them. If you constantly distract them, you are hurting their habits of concentration and focus. Be with the child and acknowledge their feelings. Tell the truth, and help them with empathy, rather than avoidance (negotiate: what are their concerns really?). Treat your  child as a person, feel with them, don’they deal with them.  Be wary of rules and advice about pregnancy. Your pregnancy and child are not projects. Your child is a lifelong relationship that will develop.rules can also give us a false sense of control. Pregnancy and childbearing are by nature a risk and cary uncertainty.  Don’t catastrophize. The story you tell yourself is the story you tell your child. Choose optimism. When pregnant and aware of the fetus’ sex, we unconsciously label and categorize or stereotype their behavior, even before born. Be wary of predertermining your child’s characteristics, and care about how you perceive your child as a fetus (are they a burden, or a joy). Feelings are feelings, never right or wrong. Be honest about how you are feeling toward your child, and acknowledge it so you can move forward accordingly. What stories are you telling yourself? Your birth will not live up to an ideal. Steer in the direction you want, and meet what comes. Debrief from the birth experience as much as you need to. Babies naturally know the “breast crawl” (finding the nipple). Skin on skin contact is important for babies. New parents need to be heard and listened to, to know that their experience is normal, that they are not monsters for their weaknesses, and that they are understood and accepted. They need this radical acceptance in order to be able to give it to their child. Not feeling for yourself impairs your ability to feel for your children. It’s not the ruptures that count, but how they are repaired, how relationships are reformed. Life will be easier for a baby if they first feel they belong, that the world is a safe place. Babies are preformed to form attachments. Foster a secure attachment style. Avoidant: you learned that others are untrustworthy and can’t be relied upon, so you stop asking for their help or reaching out. Insecure: you learned that you need to make a lot of noise to be noticed, and will probably get ignored, and can’t take companionship for granted, cause people make leave you. Dismissive: you learned that others will ask you to meet their needs, will abuse you, etc.  Babies give coercive cries, and it’s best to respond to them, so the baby knows they are safe and develops a sense of wellbeing in the world. Loneliness warns us to do something. It feels hard to tackle, because fixing it feels threatening in social situations. But try to tackle isolation and loneliness as quickly as possible. Watch out for feelings of superiority/inferiority and choose to join social groups anyway.  Trust your child to separate at their own pace. Train your child by being responsive, but increasing the time and space between their cry and coming to their aid. What matters is the relationship. It isn’t the mistake that matters, as much as it is mending it. Let your child take the lead, helping them do things for themselves, and not rescuing them. Children need a safe place to play so they are not distracted by being told “no” all the time.  Children do not need more than a few basic toys. More toys is not better. Children need free play, time to use their imagination. Help your child start their play and then back off when they are content. Age-mixed play is different from play with peers their same age, the lessons and learning are unique and helpful. Tell your child you have confidence in their ability to find something to do when they are bored. All behavior is communication. Your child will imitate your behavior. Your behavior is co-created. For young children, behavior is the only way they can communicate. The concepts of “good” and “bad” are unhelpful. Behavior is convenient or inconvenient. What is their behavior trying to say? How is their behavior co-created with yours? Behind behavior, you find feelings. Put the feelings into words. Think of the situation from the child’s perspective. If you impose your will on your child, how will you like it when they learn to impose their will on others? It’s not about winning and losing. Go with what is working in the present, rather than what you fantasize about in the future. We say, “what if…”?” And catastrophize. If what is working stops working, then make a change.  Abilities to behave well: tolerating frustration, flexibility, problem solving, seeing and feeling things from others’ points of view. Children may learn these skills at different times and stages of their life. Sometimes, children want your attention and love so much they will seek negative attention as a final attempt rather than getting no attention. If we manipulate children, we cannot be upset if they manipulate us or others. Tech by example. Bribes should be the exception, not the norm. Play is “work” for children. To teach kids the value of money, we must teach them the value of people. Rather than forcing your child to insincerity do things, teach them by example, make sure others feel well-treated by doing things yourself. Invest time positively earlier, rather than negatively later. Try writing down your child’s point of view when you find it difficult to understand or deal with. Help behavior by putting feelings into words. Naming the pain of your child will not make it worse. The “they’re tired” excuse parents make for a child’s behavior is overused, as is the “they just need attention”. Children are feeling their feelings. Thinking they are just tired is even more infuriating for the child. Many institutionalized behaviors are treatable early on by changing habits.when working through issues, collaborate with your child, brainstorm solutions, and give your child the lead. Don't be the clever one with all the answers. Have the patience to wait for your child to think, and let them develop. Children do not have tantrums because they want to. They are feeling their feelings. See ghe child from their point of view and stay calm. No one was ever healed by being shamed or feeling silly. Retaliation is punishing your child for having feelin reflect, don’t react. Young children cannot take in complex reasons. Whingeing : crying or whining. Their crying may remind you of your pain, and therefore you may subconsciously silence it and have a low tolerance for your child’s difficult feelings. Don’t lie to your children, even by omission. They will sense the atmosphere and come to their own conclusions if you don’t tell them the truth. There is an age-appropriate way to tell your child anything, so don’t withhold information that they have a right to know. Children will lie at some point, and we all do it at some point. Children witness their parents lying all the time. There is no reason that they should not lie if they see you do it. Often, children tell lies to deal with difficult parents who cannot remain calm, or to create a narrative for their feelings they don’t understand. The more we put our feelings into words, the less our children will need to lie to deal with feelings.  Don’Don’ overreact to what your child does or tells you, or they will keep it from you. If you irrationally punish your child and are more punitive, they are more likely to continue bad behavior and learn to lie.  Make a habit of being honest about your feelings and reasons for boundaries. Don’t make up reasons for family rules or other boundaries, or try to hide your feelings or lie about them. Be flexible and willing to see every child as an individual. Define yourself (your own feelings). Reason wars are “fact tennis”, and they don’t work because everyone has a different experience and need to be taken seriously from their point of view. When you are more considerate of your child, they become more considerate of you.  Define the problem, find the feelings behind the problem, brainstorm solutions. Keep naming your own feelings, because they really are the reason you do what you do and put down boundaries. Make the first move to repair the rupture. Update your expectations about their willingness to welcome your advice, etc. Help kids get the help they need, don’don’ label people as bad. The “bad lot” of kids you blame are someone else’s kid who is trying their best.  Teenagers are more likely to collaborate with you and be helpful if they feel like you are part of your life, that you spend time together and are in the same realm. How your child feels 
about and and sees themself will always , to some extent, be influenced by how you respond to them, how you delight in them, how you greet them, how you accept them. It is never too late to try to repair a rupture.
