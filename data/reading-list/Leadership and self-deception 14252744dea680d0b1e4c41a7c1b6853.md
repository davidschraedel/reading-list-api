Notes: A book about why people are sometimes jerks. Self-deception keeps us from seeing and solving problems. When people are criticized, they rarely agree with the criticism. The bigger problem is when we can’t see that we have a problem. When we are stuck in our own experience, “stuck in the box”, in self-deception. There is a germ in organizations, the disease of self-deception. People care most about how others regard them, not what they do or say. How much you care is what matters most in influence. If you do things for you and not them, they know. In the box: our needs are primary, others second or trivial, we see others as objects, not people. We get in the box through self-betrayal, betraying our own sense of what we should do for others. Act contrary to what we feel we should do for another = self-betrayal. We can be hard on the problem, and still be out of the box and see someone as a human being. It’s not what we do necessarily, but how we do it. Justifications are self-betrayal. When we are in the box, we don’t see the truth, that people really are people like us, that their concerns are just as reasonable as ours. In our self-betrayal, we minimize our own faults, don’t see ourself clearly, don’t see others clearly, and increase the perceived catastrophy of our own concerns. Self-betrayl 1. When we act contrary to how we feel we should treat another human. 2. When we betray ourselves we see the world in a way that justifies our betrayal. 3. When we when I see the world in a self-justified way, my view of reality becomes distorted. 4. Our distortion means we are in the box. 5. over time, certain boxes become characteristic of me and I carry them with me. We inflate the others’ faults, our own virtues, the value of things that justify our self-betrayal, and blame. The box is a blame-space. In the box, our way of being is blaming. But are others truly to blame? Is that really the whole reality, seeing the world clearly? What really caused our irritation? Our self-betrayal. When I betray myself, I become self-deceived by entering the box. Before we betray ourselves, we see others simply as they are through our feelings of how to treat other humans. When we carry boxes or self-justifying images with us, we don’t have to have feelings of betray to be in the box, because we already are. We are already not seeing reality as it is, what is really true. Others’ problems don't excuse ours, that is a justification. Being in the box provokes others to be in the box. When we feel justified in mistreating others, they feel unjustly mistreated, and justify themselves. Our box needs us to justify ourselves, asking for more poor behavior from others so that we feel justified. What the box needs most is justification. In the box, we need others to be a problem. A disappointment, so we can justify our current pains, our self-betrayal. The box is destructive, creating the problems we blame others for. It's almost as if we collided to make trouble. When multiple people are in the box causing each other problems, we call it collusion. By being in the box, we Provoke the behaviors we hate most.  Our box (justification) is at odds with results and productivity. In the box, we focus on ourselves only, not true productivity. We can’t see this in the box, and Provoke other, and spread the propagation of lying to ourselves. One person in the box in an organization end up spreading the delight in others’ failures. It creates a culture of sick people. Don't be the doctor that spreads the germs. Self-betrayal creates self-deception, creating many other symptoms that we call people problems. How we get out of the box: when you want to be out of the box, you are already in that moment. What we worry about is how to stay out. How do we continue to see others as people? Don't try to change others, because we put ourselves in the box, not others. Don't cope with others (continuing to blame them). Don't leave others (the box goes with you). Don’t communicate (communicating in the box is unproductive). It's not the skill or technique, it's the way you do, the way you see. Your behavior? No, outward behavior in the box is just poorly-executed box behavior. “What do we do?” Is the wrong question. Question your own virtues. We never question our virtue when “in the blx”. How to stay out? Act on your impulses to treat them as humans. Do what you feel you should for others. This does not mean we do every good thing in the world, or add obligations to our life, but the people around us, those we encounter and connect with are people we must treat well. We cannot do the ideal at all times, but we must not justify ourselves, but instead know that we are doing the best we can, and still seeing others and things as they truly are. Allow others to be in the box without blaming them. You need to be a leader, to lead by example and not coercion. Be free of self-betrayal to be a leader people want to follow and become like.  Remember that we don't need blame, because we all contribute. Refuse to blame others, and instead accept responsibility. Others will be happier to work with you. Remember: learn how you can be more helpful to others, when you are in the box, you can't focus on results, your influence and success depend on being out of the box, you get out of the box by ceasing to resist other people, Don't try to be perfect but try to be better, use the principles in your life, don't look for others’ boxes, don't accuse others of being in the box, don’t deny your being in the box, focus on what you can do right to help, worry about whether you are helping others. Companies fail for the same reasons families do. We don't know those around us until we leave the box and join them. As far as I am concerned, the problem is me. We know about self-deception because we see it in others, but we are usually not skilled at seeing it in ourselves. Outward mindset = out of the box.  Change in an organization starts with the leader, as they accept responsibility.
