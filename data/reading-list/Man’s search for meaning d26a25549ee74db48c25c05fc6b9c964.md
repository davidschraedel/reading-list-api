# Man’s search for meaning

Author: Viktor e frankl
Date Finished: July 8, 2024

Notes: Man does not simply exist, but decides next what he will be. A human being has freedom to take a stand toward conditions, but not freedom from conditions. Every human being has the freedom to change at any instant. We are capable of changing ourselves and other things for the better. How can we dare to predict the behavior of man? A human being is not one thing among others, not a machine. Man has potentialities. Man invented the gas chambers, and also yet is also they that entered the gas chambers uprightly with God on his lips. Tragic Optimism. How is it possible to say yes to life, in spite of “all that”? Make the best of any situation. Best as in optimal, or optimistic aim. A human being is not one in pursuit of happiness, but a reason to be happy. Happiness is not pursued, but ensued. The existential vacuum or collective neurosis of our time can be rightly described as a private and personal form of nihilism.  Psychotherapy can be problematic in it’s assertions, becoming a symptom of theass neurosis/nihilism and not a solution. For example, there is a danger in teaching that man is nothing but a product or result of environment and biology, that he is the victim of elements beyond his control or outside influences. This neurotic fatalism is fostered and strengthened by a psychotherapy that denies that man is free. Pandeterminism = the view of man that disregards his capacity to take a stand toward any conditions whatsoever. Man is not fully conditioned or determined, but rather determines himself  whether he gives in to conditions or stands up to them.  He decides what he will become in the next moment.  Freedom is in danger of  degenerating into mere arbitrariness unless it is lived in terms of responsibleness.  If a human is not more than a damaged brain machine, for what purpose should people serve as psychiatrists or therapists? What man becomes, he has made of himself. Tragic triad = pain, guilt, death.    Saying yes to life, presupposes that life is potentially meaningful under any conditions, even those which are most miserable. What matters is to make the best of any situation.  Faith, love, and hope, cannot be ordered or forced.  The drug scene is one aspect of a more general phenomenon of  the feeling of meaningless resulting from the frustration of our existential needs.  People have enough to live by, but nothing to live for.  Man does not live by welfare alone.  Meaninglessness is the proof of one’s humanness, though it may cause a pathological reaction. Depression, aggression, addiction, are three facets of the mass neurotic syndrome. Not each and every case is a direct result of meaninglessness, but it may be overcome if there is a meaning worth living for.  Who can guarantee that a larger meaning will not present itself later on in your life? You will have to carry on and see in order to find out.  Meaning is changed by context, by each frame in a moving picture. The final meaning of life presents itself at the end, on the verge of death, it cannot be known now. Creating meaning in a life is done in one of three main ways: creating a work or doing a deed, experiencing something or encountering someone, to rise above or consider suffering enobling/enabling or turning predicaments into human achievements.  Meaning is down-to-earth, becoming aware as to what can be done about a situation. Conscience directs within a hiearchy of values, built in the evolution of our species.   One may find meaning in suffering. We benefit from captivity when we see it as a growth experience.  “I broke my neck, it didn’t break me”.  Without my suffering,  the growth I have achieved would have been impossible.  Meaning is available through suffering. Unnecessary suffering is masochistic.  We dehumanizing mistaken people or convicts, etc., by explaining their error and reasoning away the mystery of their mistake., seeing them as a machine to be fixed, rather than a free human. To be seen as a human who can choose wrong, and then rise above guilt and become a better person is more meaningful and empowering.  Collective guilt is totally irresponsible, to hold one person responsible for the behavior of another.   Live as if you are living for the second time, and acted as wrongly the first time as you are about to act now. Everything is errevocably  stored and treasured from the past, nothing lost.  Young people should envy the old, not pity them.  Our society adores the young, those who are “useful”. Nihilism does not contain that there isnnothing, but that everything is meaningless.  It is a learned meaninglessness.  Everything will bec9me still worse, unless each of us does his/her best.  Dostoevsky: am I worthy of my suffering? - What does life expect of me? Each is irreplaceable, with unique demands and abilities. Thus each has a distinct why for their life. A man who knows he bears responsibility toward something else has found his why, and cannot throw away his life, despite the how.
