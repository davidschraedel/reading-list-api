# How to know a person

Author: David Brooks
Date Finished: March 26, 2024
Finished: Yes

Notes: Our posture toward the world: what we look for, what we project out onto the world. A physical and mental presence that we bring to others. What is our gaze/expression that we bring to others? We find what we seek in this posture. When we look for beauty, we find wonders. Attention is a moral act, because it creates and brings into being. Active curiosity, a posture of love toward others, is the way to know others. Knowing is a whole body experience (emotion, intellection, touching, thinking). One of our delusions is that any person can be qualified in some way, or classified by some term. Any man manifests certain things at one point and others at another. The same man, but different at different times. Labels are not useful. How does this unique person encounter this other unique person. Morality is about how you pay attention to others. The essential immoral act is the inability to see others. Love is knowledge of the individual. Accompaniment = doing something next to someone. Float down the river with them. Let other people be themselves, open to whatever may come. Be lingerable: the type of person people like to linger with. Willingness, not willfulness. Accompaniment is about presence. You don’t have to say something to someone in a hard time, but you do have to be there. Negative effort: the ability to hold back and let things flow, being lingerable. People need to know that you respect their stuff, that you respect their holding back. Playfulness, people are more human when they play. Each person processes events differently because of what they experience and interpret in their past and present. A person’s whole perspective, how they see the world, can be transformed. We see things as we are, not as the things are. We have only subjective access to an objective reality. Seeing is a process or prediction/projection on what we actually see and perceive. It is extremely flawed. The art of hearing others. Be a loud listener. A good conversation is an exploration. Create a communal effort or collective discovery in the conversation. Treat attention as an on/off switch, be all in. Active listening is an invitation to others to express. Favor familiarity, people like to talk about things they know or are attached to. Make people authors not witnesses, help people really revisit the moment, what their experience was like. Ask specific questions about how the details of what they experienced, and how they experienced it. Don’t fear the pause, take time to reflect before responding, because you can’t listen while you are preparing to respond. A good conversationalist is a midwife. A midwife doesn't give birth, she assists the other in their creation. Sometimes it takes another to help us see our own personal truths. They can help us get distance from problems we are too immersed in to see. Job interviews: Will a person be kind/generous, are they skilled in one thing. Most people don’t escape high school. What were they like in high school, how did they interact with and see others? Kids aren’t afraid of blunt questions. Asking questions can be a vulnerable thing. In a world of security, we ask fewer questions, we assume and don’t venture into discomfort and uncertainty. Perspective receiving, not perspective taking. Worst questions are evaluative (closed questions, questions that imply “i’m about to judge you”). Instead, surrender power, give freedom to go as deep or shallow as possible, open-ended, but not vague and uncaring. People long to be asked questions about who they are. Ask big questions that lift people out of the ruts they are in (why you? Why is that a problem for you? What’s working really well in your life? What do you keep postponing? What would you do if you weren’t afraid? What is the best way to grow old? What crossroads are you at? If you died tonight, what would you regret not doing? If we meet a year from now, what will we be celebrating? If the bext five years is a chapter in your life, what is that chapter about? Can you be yourself where you are and still fit in? What have you said yes to that you no longer believe in? What forgiveness are you withholding? How have you contributed to the problem you are trying to solve? What is the gift you currently hold in exile? What are you most selfconfident about? Have you ever been solitary without being lonely? Which of your five senses is strongest? What has become clearer to you as you’ve aged?). People are a lot more willing to be vulnerable than you think. People long to be asked questions about who they are. People always talk if you listen. Few have ever truly been listened to in their lives. The lonely become bad actors, those unrecognized and unsoothed and unincluded become the few who are “problematic”, we create our “enemies”.  Every conversation operates upon an underlining frame or prerequisite perspective. We should spend less time reverting to our own frames or arguing about frames and more time operating from their frame and seeking to understand it. Those with less power have more awareness of how others see or listen to them. We project our individual mental experiences into the world and thereby mistake our mental perception to be the world. We see affordances. Our power expands our sense of capacity/capability, or field of influence in perception, what we look for. The illuminator model: morality is a social practice, character is not gained alone, people need to be and feel seen by others or be illuminated. What matters in this model is how skillful someone is in interacting with others, in these tasks of illuminating and learning moral interaction/connection with others. We construct/organize our stories about ourselves, only when prompted to do so. By asking others to tell their stories, we help them craft and build their narrative. They find the patterns as we ask them to. People don’t look to their posterity if they don’t look back to their ancestors. The presence of the past, how the dead live in us. How do your ancestors show up in your life? Those with wisdom are like coaches, who really see you, and help you process your emotions and experiences. They prod you along until you understand for yourself. They create an atmosphere of sharing, exchange, seeing, compassion. Wisdom is about reflection and connection with others. Friends hold up a mirror, helping us discover ourselves. Wisdom is a social practice, a community of truth. If equal affection cannot be, let the more loving one be me. Attention is a moral act. It brings things into being. Evil happens when people are unseen, when we fail to see the humanity in others. Truly seeing others is one way we live virtuously, and paying them the right kind of attention is how we truly love.
