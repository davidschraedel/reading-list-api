Notes: We can’t help but pace ourselves, guess how much we have left in us. Our endurance is not defined by how much we actually have left in us, but how much further we THINK or believe we can go. Our head can hold us back or help us forward. Perception of limits is what keeps us back or pushes us forward to ultimate endurance. Being able to keep our mind from wandering, like the marshmallow test, impulse inhibition matters. Response inhibition seems to be a finite resource that depletes when used heavily and restores later. These skills are likely both born, and made through practice. Mental endurance is a key part of physical endurance. There are physical needs involved, but sports psychology is a huge factor.  Pain: without pain, you can’t manage or pace yourself. You need some pain. Many top athletes can endure a lot of pain, and may even enjoy it. You run your shin after bumping it to cause endorphins that crowd out the painful feelings.
