Notes: Creating trust, ordered by importance: 1) benevolence, 2) honesty/integrity, 3) competence. Halo effect: we are more likely to trust people who our friends or acquaintances trust. The IKEA effect, hazing, and sunk cost fallacy: we place more value on what we have put effort into. Vulnerability precedes trust, by creating vulnerability loops of vulnerability and acknowledgement. You need to find a problem big enough that others' help is needed. It reminded me of family, because having children is a problem where both parents no longer can do it themselves, and the first child is totally new, so they have to rely on each other to make it work and be humble or notice when the other person needs help and do something about it, because otherwise the family effort doesn’t succeed. When intimacy/vulnerability loops are completed, Oxytocin is released. We bond through vulnerability and acceptance of and a helping hand extended toward others’ vulnerability. The IKEA effect is the opposite of impressing or winning people over. Create a problem big enough that requires everyone’s support. At the influencers dinner, this is preparing a meal in less than an hour so that everyone attended can eat. Suddenly everyone is involved, and needed, and since few would be experts, vulnerability loops are frequent. CONNECTING: we connect with people who are most familiar. The most familiar in our life is ourself. So we tend to connect with those who are most similar to ourselves. In dating apps, anything that reminds us of ourselves becomes appealing. The Mere Exposure Effect: being exposed to something causes us to like it more. This is why when you listen to a mediocre song 20 times, it starts to become enjoyable. This is the only reason the world cares about the Mona Lisa. The more people have in common, the more likely they are to connect. We have multiplex relationships. A plex is one commonality. People who we connect with that are different from us often have many more plexs that are common with us. We end up with our friends because they are like us, and they live nearby. We more often connect with people that are closer in distance. Even with desks in an office, the people with closer desks are more likely to connect and communicate. We feel comfortable with what we know, so we often model our parents’ careers, etc.  Belonging includes : membership, influence, integration and fulfillment of needs, shared emotional connection.  Member ship includes boundaries between in and out group members, emotional safety, identification to belong, personal investment or something to do, and a common symbol or such. People need to have influence in the group, some way to participate. If they can’t contribute, then they don’t have influence, and it’should a one-way relationship. (Need to rally around something big enough that everyone’s input and help is needed).  It doesn’t have to be grandiose,  just a give-and-take. Our minds take shortcuts, mental biases. We can design a path to deal with the consequences of these biases, a path to direct the elephant, so the rider does not have to do heavy lifting. Most businesses start with the invitation/ad/pitch/discovery, but that is backwards (start with the end in mind). Membership, engagement, then discovery. What do we want people to feel/think/do? What will lead to this type of membership? What attracts the right people to engage in this way? when we offer from the place of membership and our values, people self-select in or out. We don’t start driving without a place in mind and just hope to end up somewhere nice. Look at the end result (membership) and ask yourself what kind of membership you want to create. How do you want people to interact with you?  In discovery, Create curiosity with information gaps (that are neither too large or too small).  Leave them with a manageable curiosity, raising some questions, but not overwhelming uncertainty or uninteresting ambiguity.  Just be sure that the experience meets expectations and is worth it. Don’t click bait people, or lie, as it will ruin your reputation. If I shared with a participant, someone i’m not friends with, every aspect of the design of the experience, would they feel manipulated or would they appreciate the thoughtfulness?  Always state if you have a brand involved. Protect the community, and don’t agree to bad deals with external organizations like brands, etc. Stay true to your values. If you truly have the participant’s best interest at heart, the messiness of your first events and the flaws in your approach can often be overlooked, and create a vulnerable state for others to join. Growing pains are good if you are benevolent. Trust in your competence can be built if you are honest, and benevolent. Try giving people a way to unwind and some transition time to make starting an engagement or gathering less awkward, by giving them some job to  do or giving a tour. Don’t leave them to their own devices, help them get the ball rolling, make it easy to participate, easy to attend.  Big parties: if you had only 10% the budget, how would you accomplish the same goal? 10% of that? Etc. Until you get to the heart of the matter.  There are always creative solutions that can help build more meaningful connections. The may require more thinking but tend to be less expensive. “How can we accomplish the same business impact with far less logistics and expense?” hiring celebrities can create a need to outdo the last activity, to get someone even bigger. Make sure celebrities are on-brand, and consider making them a surprise, even getting lesser-known talent as a hipster move or mutually beneficial collaboration. Make sure talent is not always the same style, switch it up to avoid needing to top your last event. But again, what does this talent have to do with your brand? Your competitor can likely hire the same talent. Choose wisely, have a purpose, a connection, a reason they represent and facilitate the connection between customers and your brand.
