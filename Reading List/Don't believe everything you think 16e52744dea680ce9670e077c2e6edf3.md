# Don't believe everything you think

Notes: Truth is simple, exeprienced. Two arrows: the first is our experience, the second our reaction. We live through our own perception of the world. We therefore live in a world of thought, not reality. Our thought creates our “reality”. True reality is the objective present without attached meaning. Any meaning or thinking we give something is self-created and our choice. It’s not events, but our interpretation of them that causes us good or bad feelings. Our thinking about events is the cause of our pain. If external events were solely responsible for our level of happiness, we would be happy a lot more often and less often than we would assume. You wouldn’t be sad in your room alone, sad while watching TV, sad while spending time with other people, sad at the gym, etc. Internal influences are a much bigger part of the picture than we assume. Must a particular experience lead to suffering? Would everyone in that situation feel the same about it? No. How would you feel if you didn’t think that you hate____? Without our usual thinking, our experience is completely altered. The root cause of our suffering is our own thinking. By changing our thinking, we change our reality. Our experience is not inevitable. We are one thought away from transforming our life experience. Serenity is the following: event + no thinking = reality/peace. Without my reaction, nothing has the power to make me angry. What really causes you pain? You do. True freedom is the ability to unattached to whatever happens in the mind. What happens around us is like an empty boat floating toward us. There is no one to blame, no one steering the “disturbance”. The mind evolved to think, to keep us alive. Much of this survival thinking costs us peace and happiness. We navigate the modern world with a mind programmed with primitive fears. You are not a product of your environment, but a co-creator with it. Thoughts = neutral observations, insights, or intuitive promptings that enter our mind. Thinking = our judgment or opinion we have about our thoughts. Thought is a noun, thinking a verb (something we do). Thoughts are intrinsically neutral, but our thinking takes us on an emotional journey with thoughts. The usefulness of thinking is an illusion that causes us to experience negative, unwanted emotions. Thoughts create, thinking often destroys. The idea is to sit with the spontaneous thoughts springing up, as this is creativity. [Does it not matter what question prompts our thoughts though?]. Our emotions tell us whether we are experiencing thought, or thinking (ruminating). As you begin thinking, you’ll feel a cascade of negative emotions. Our natural state is joy: love, compassion, healing.  Our goal is not to stop feeling negative emotions, because emotions are signals. The goal is to decrease the amount of time it takes us to regulate and return to a state of peace. While positive thought can lead to positive emotion, it is not required. You don’t feel happy only when you have positive thoughts, hero when you think positively (which would be a chore if that were the case). Positive emotion is solely the byproduct of being fully in the present moment and connected to life, rather than thinking about it. Our natural state of being is joy, love, and peace. Children exemplify this, naturally being in a state of openness, curiosity, laughter, wonder. The times you are most stressed, you are thinking most. The times you are most calm and at peace, you are thinking least. It’s not the content of our thinking, but that, or how much, we are thinking. Thinking takes us away from the present into the past or future. You do not have to try to be present or thinking positively. More often, the solution is removal, not addition.  [As we get older, we learn to think and have much more ro think about. We spend less time experiencing and more time thinking, of necessity to some degree, but also as a learned habit]. Don’t seek the external to feel inside, because feelings are created within. We don’t therefore have to do anything to minimize our consuming thought. Simply become aware and let go. A process of flow, not force. Stop resisting and avoiding. What we resist persists. What we accept and let be will leave inevitably.  If we let our mind sit  without fighting  it, the dirt settles to the bottom. Acknowledge your thinking and give it the space to pass without judgment, and slowly the mind will clear again.  To stop thinking: PAUSE. Pause, take some deep breaths. Ask, asking yourself “does this thinking help me feel the way i want to?”. Understand, that you have the choice to let go of your thinking. Say, repeating to yourself “thinking is the root cause of suffering”.  Experience feeling your emotions fully.  Don’t we need thinking to survive in the world when you enter flow state, how much thinking is going on? It’s minimal to none. Our flow is not about thinking or “judgment”, it is a natural state of intuition and creativity and presence. The thoughts that come are neutral, constructive, creative. We really do do our best work in a state of non-thinking.  Athletes enter “the zone” in peak performance. They don’t think, they act on instinct based on former practice and formed habits in the subconscious.  We may attribute our flow to a particular activity when we are in non-thinking. This creates a false belief that that activity alone is when we can feel flow or fully present. We can be in flow anytime we are in the present moment.  The divine is in the present only. “I am”, not i was, or i will be. You cannot think your way into flow or love. They are an occurance, a byproduct, of the present.  As with thoughts and thinking, where our goals and dreams come from will determine whether they make us suffer or how we feel pursuing them. Do they come from our higher self, or are they the product of our ego or “thinking”. Two sources: goals created out of inspiration, and goals created out of desperation. Desperation goals create 
feelings of immense scarcity and urgency , imposter syndrome, self doubt, being short on time, and even if we accomplish  the goal, shortly after we nginue ti feel discontented, lacking, and compare ourselves to others. These desperation goals are means goals, not “ends” goals. They aim to get something from the goal, to let something external fix the internal. Example: become famous, to be valued. Unless we fix the underlying feeling of not being valued  or not being good enough, the external goal will never change how you feel. Desperation goals leave us feeling emptier than before, so we set even bigger goals, etc. Inspiration goals: creating because we feel moved, inspired, expansive. It feels like a calling, not obligation. We feel like a force of life comes from within , pulled instead of forced, from a place of abundance. What did you dream of doing when you were a kid we naturally have inspiration goals all the time as adults. This creation is an overflowing of love and joy for life. We create because we want to. This feeling of inspiration comes through us from something greater than ourselves. We call it “divine inspiration”, because these ideas of what we want to create seem far greater   bigger than we could come up with on our own. In this state, we feel whole, complete, unconditional love, etc. Most of us suppress this divine inspiration with thinking, that creates self-doubt. Loving a life of non-thinking means we create goals out of inspiration not desperation. How do we know which is which? They look similar, but are different under the surface. Goals and dreams created from thought are of inspiration, while goals and dreams from thinking are of desperation. Desperation goals usually have conditional happiness only via achievemen, rooted in fear. Goals of inspiration comes from what you value. Most of the time, inspiration goals will lead to greater happiness, and may achieve some of the desperation goals’ aims, but there are no good or bad goals. We still need financial goals, and to live and survive.   The same aims can be based on inspiration or desperation. Ask yourself “what does ____ (making money)  give me or allow me to do that I value most?”. We don’therefore drop everything to achieve our inspiration goals. Think “everyday” rather than a time quota or how much. What are you doing everyday to support your goal of creating a happy family? It’s not about if, but how you can make it happen. As with thoughts, inspiration dreams and goals just pop up and come naturally. We don’therefore need to do extra work to find these aspirations. If I had infinite money, no fear, and didn't need to receive any recognition, what would I do or create? We learned to stuff this inspiration. A mind without thinking has no limits. Good and bad: a piano has no bad keys. Only in the context of a piece do certain keys sound unlikeable or unnatural. There is not one correct path. Don’t label paths as wrong or right, stay open to opportunities. There are no wrong spots to pause on a mountain. Instead of l[king for right or wrong, look for truth without judgment. Look for universal truths. These truths are not subjective, and are rarely at the extremes.  A wise person is always unlearning.  Intuition leads us to who we want to be. Your intuition is usually the choice that makes you feel a tinge of fear because it requires you to step into the unknown to create a new reality rather than stay in your familiar one.  Nothing that grows can stay the same, especially your. We live in an illusion of certainty. Most of what we have can be taken away in a moment. We know the only constant is change, yet we spend our energy fighting it. Relax into uncertainty. Accept things as they are, instead of how you think they should be. Trust you will be ok no matter what happens. We can’therefore control all that happens, but we can choose our response. Our job is determining what we want, not necessarily how we get it. Delegate the “hiw” to your intuition because there are infinite ways to get what you want. We will be given the next step. The pathis only revealed as we begin walking. Fwad stops us from heeding our intuition. Following your intuition may sometimes make others uncomfortable because they are not ready for you to change. Don’t sacrifice your peace for their temporary peace. Are you making decisions to feel safe, or to feel free?  If your mind is full of old ideas, you won’t have space for new thoughts. New thoughts are necessary for the change you seek.  Asking questions that challenge our current thinking is one of the best ways to create space in our minds. For peak performance athletes, intense rest happens after intense training. Strength is created in the rest. Nietzsche took walks for mental space, Einstein played violin. Space is like rest, we need it for our new ideas and mental wellness. What we do not question controls us, what we question frees us. But am I just lazy? Nonthinking is our most productive state. Slow down. If you’ve been living life in fight or flight, you may be uncomfortable without those feelings. This is a growing pain that will dissipate. Practicing nonthinkin does not mean avoiding reality, but accepting reality as it really is rather than what we think it should be. It is a space of nonjudgment.using thoughts instead of thinking. Channel your energy into your goals of inspiration instead of thinking. Create an activation ritual or a morning routine that helps you get into flow or nonthinking. An object in motion will stay in motion. To the person who is at peace with the feeling of fear, the whole universe yields. Unconditional love. Courage is the presence of love in the midst of fear. Others may critique or tell us we are not enough, they are simply caught up in their own thinking. You don’t have to listen. Live via your intuition. You already have all you want or could dream of within you. The only thing stopping us from doing what we truly desire is our fear. Question what it is that you are truly afraid of. Anxiety is an illusion designed to keep us in our comfort zone: nothing more.
