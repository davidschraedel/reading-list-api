# Real artists don’t starve

Author: Jeff goins
Finished: No

Rating: ⭐⭐⭐
Notes: Take small leaps, small steps. Don’t quit your job, just do something everyday, make small meaningful bits of art, parts of your craft. We are either becoming more of our true selves, or something else. Life is not static. Always strive to reinvent yourself. Let go of the starving artist mentality. The most famous artists did not starve. The ancient masters and artists were praised for copying the work of their predecessors, a sign of their ability. Copying other’s work is being a student. After we are students, we can be masters and then steal like an artist, making something original from those we borrow from. We find our voice by mimicking the voice of others. You are not an artist because you steal, you steal because you are an artist (you’re not a copycat, you internalize a craft, and your style and voice emerges). Cite your sources. Don’t vet lost in the pursuit of originality. You know you’ve done your job, when you are no longer the thief, but the one being stolen and borrowed from. Apprentice under a master. Apprentice yourself. Get a mentor and be humble enough to learn, but tenacious enough to get their acceptance. Be flexible on the details, but stubborn on the vision. Don’t be insane, doing the same thing over and over again. You can't succeed on your own. Iterate and learn from others. Often success is not skill but tenacity and perseverance. You need a patron. Good art is subjective. We need people to tell us and others what is good or not. You need a patron or influener that makes things happen. You need an audience of one fist, and this makes success all the more likely. The patron must vouch for your success. You need an advocate that believes in your vision. You need to cultivate your patron. Where are these patrons, tastemakers? Closer than you think. All around us, rhere are patrons that are willing to support us. We must be teachable. Influencers love to invest in others, so make it easy on them. Be willing to learn. Show them you are applying their advice. Make good things, make it convenient and easy to connect with a patron, and be willing to learn from them. Use your day job as a patron, as a means to an end, a support for your art. Learn skills from your job, let it fund your craft. Pursuing patrons (an evangelist): seek out masters, all around you. Patrons need artists to support. Genius is a place, not a person. A network. If they don’t let you join, start yoir own creative space, and others that appreciate you will join you. Embrace your place. Put yourself where creativity is happening. Earn the attention of those already seen by serving them. Artist = visionary that brings people together. You must be findable. Practice in public. Always work for something. If you find yourself holding a grudge against your clients, or upset with them. Charge more for your work. Don’t make a habit of working for free. Stand up for yourself. Be your own patron.
