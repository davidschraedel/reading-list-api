Notes: Rule 1: make it really hard. Rule 2: don’t die. Constant comfort is a radically new experience for humans. We are swimming in comfort. We experience 1st world stress, whereas our ancestors experienced life-or-death discomfort. We are less likely to die, but less healthy. Humans see gray, based on all the inputs we have perceived before. As we experience fewer problems, we lower our threshold for what we see as problems. We become less and less satisfied with what we now see. We are terrible at noticing this. Humans used to live wild, hunt, be close to the plants and animals they ate. Most of us have no idea what’s out on the edge of our potential. Misogi: What are you mentally and spiritually willing to put yourself through to become a better human? There should be a 50/50 chance of success. Fail half the time, or it’s not difficult enough. Failure 100 years ago meant you might die. But we vastly overestimate failure today, and underestimate the benefits. There’s adventure in the hero’s journey, struggling, internal locus, and using the tools you have. Native peoples have long held traditions of rites of passage rituals. 3 key stages: separation (venturing alone), transition (challenging battle/task), incorporation (reintegrating as a matured human). Rites of passage are largely gone from our modern world. There exists in all of us an archetypal need to be initiated. Misogi is a challenge that’s you against you. It’s not about comparison. Therefore, don’t post or share online, etc. about misogis. Some degree of challenge in life, that is not overwhelming, leads to increased resilience. Newness slows down our sense of time. This is why our childhood seemed slow. Everything was new to us. Getting outside of our comfort zone slows time, makes us aware of details. Most people say they would like to live in the country, but that’s least best for survival. Why would we want to live in open spaces? We are built for tribes of about no more than  150 people. The higher the density  of the population, the lower chance of happiness. Rediscover boredom, preferably outside, for minutes, hours, days. Boredom is a desire for desire, it’s a motivational state. Time in unfocused mode is critical. Resting is an effort. Constant attention can overwork our brain, it can be overdone. Wandering mind is necessary. Boredom helps you rest and reset. Constant focus and attention is like feeding our minds junkfood. Digital devices are a large part of our problem with unbroken distraction.  Your attention is the goal of these digital lab experiments.  We have slot machines in our pockets. We should learn to deal with boredom. Boredom increases creativity, by teaching you to think about doing things, to teach you to think on a different wavelength. Dealing with boredom is creativity. We are facing a creativity crisis. Steve Jobs believed in boredom, and didn’t let his own kids use the iPad. Instead of “less phone”, think “more boredom”. Given the choice, humans choose something they can control or predict. We evolved to look to the future and track information to help us survive. Nature is a necessary connection in our life for thriving. “Nature bathing”, or spending time immersed in nature, helps us heal in each area of our being. Biophilia is increasingly considered seriously as an antidote to our being stressed and overworked. Passing through nature is as easy as passing through the park on the way to work. Stop thinking of nature as “out there” somewhere else. Nature is a plant, your backyard, the lawn, park, hills, trees, etc. Spend time in all types of nature, especially wild nature. The wilder the nature, the better.  3 days in the wild is like a meditation retreat, the 3 day effect is notable in performance afterwards and continues.  We struggle with sleep, partly because we never spend enough time in adequate darkness and silence, and don’t physically exert ourselves enough.  We tend to decrease our senses in many areas (wearing shoes, etc.), but we increase our stimulus in hearing. We are in constant noise, and find “silence” uncomfortable. Silence-induced discomfort is a new learned behavior. More noise increases stress and anxiety. Even in anechoic chambers, we hear sound, because we make sound. There is sound everywhere, but we have increasingly drowned out the natural sounds of our world with loud noise, and are less aware of the sound that would otherwise be noticeable. Anechoic chambers and natural silence help us recalibrate to “silence” and lower our blood pressure and stress.  At h9ke silence is more relaxing than listening to Mozart, etc. Natural silence is better for us than most of the “relaxing” solutions we are sold. Most people could lose wight as well, but don’t because of the discomfort. Most on a “diet” stop after 5 weeks, because that is the average time when people feel the most discomfort, when their biology is telling them to get back to comfort to stay alive. We eat when we need reward hunger, a psychological reward for eating, more sonwhen we are stressed.  Early humans had few ways to store food, so they stored it in fat.  Real hunger drives just 25% of eating. Ask yourself “why are you eating?” Rather than if you are eating something specific at a specific time. Many people don’t know how to deal with stress. People who stay physically healthy simply choose a walk over ice cream, etc.  We have a lot more chronic stress than acute stress. We use ultraprocessed food as a cheep self-medication, to deal with the low-level chronic stress of day-to-day.  Stress eating is the reason most fad diets don’t work. Binging on a good diet doesn’t help. Keep track of what you eat, so you are accountable, watch yourself so you feel accountable. Eat unprocessed whole grains and tubers, fruits and vegetables, and low-ish fat animal protein. Unprocessed grains must be cooked in water to become edible (rice, quinoa, oatmeal).  Don’t eat into Supreme comfort each meal.  We no longer have the stress of lean times, of going without food.  We don’t gain weight in small consistent gains, but we also don’t gain it in big bursts. We tend to gain weight at particular important times in our life with stress (getting married, moving, holidays, etc.).  Our ancestors didn’t snack around the clock.  Lean times are a necessary part of optimizing health. When we burn tissue, we burn old tissue, like cancer, etc. The old cells in our body are related to lots of diseases.   Autophagy is natural at night during sleep. If you eat before bed, you don’t let your body take out the trash during sleep. For our ancestors, food was likely consumed during daylight hours, leaving long periods of fasting during the night. Hunger can be better than many things we try. Breakfast is not the most important meal. Embrace the discomfort of hunger. Think about your death everyday. The easier our lives get, the more uncomfortable we seem to become with death.  We act as if life is a checklist, a list of things to accomplish. The checklist is never-ending. This is the same reason we struggle with materialism. Mindfulness is the answer to this issue. Everyone is dying right now. To ignore this, is ignorance. Be Mindful of your death, that you are walking toward http://it.to think of death is the necessary tool for preparing for your deathbed. Greed, anger, and ignorance are the three main delusions. When our mind is not cared for, these delusions come up. Impermanence = nothing lasts, and therefore nothing can be held onto. All things pass away, all dies. Our false sense of permanence is the reason we put off doing the things we want to do.  People who think about their death are kinder and more charitable to both themselves and others. Thinking about death increases gratitude, thinking of what might not be, and what therefore is. Life is a privilege. Think of death in the morning, mid-day, and evening. Nature is cruel. The hunter often gives an animal a fast and painless death.  Carry the load. Non-western/non-industrialized peoples get lots of exercise, and burn more calories doing that exercise than industrialized peoples do. Many people in our society do hardly any exercise at all. Hunting and foraging is not just a physical exercise, but mental work. In the wild, we don’t lift perfectly measured weights, use padded weight machines, bulk up, or watch TV. The decisions and perseverance and mental aptitude necessary in real life, outdoor exercise is what keeps our minds sharp. Uneven ground forces us to burn more calories, to do more work.  Humans were made for carrying.  Carrying was likely more common than running. Most of us almost never carry anything difficult. It’s different than a marathon or a backpack or a weight room. Rucksack. Ruck is a verb, for those with rucksacks. Like the military, you always carry weight. 50 pounds is the most weight a soldier can thrive with. Rucking is strength and aerobics 101.
