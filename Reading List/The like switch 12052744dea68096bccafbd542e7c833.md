Notes: Proximity, frequency, duration, intensity: the friendship formula/factors. Proximity: be in the same place, establishing yourself as non-threatening. What makes a stranger worthy of our attention? What to approach and what to avoid? We send friend or foe signals, such as the urban scowl. Street beggers look for friend signals. This is why people develop an urban scowl. Your brain is monitoring verbal and nonverbal communication. Only when they become abnormal do we take extra notice, like a car that tailgates. Tech reduces the time we spend practicing social interaction and reading and sending out signals. Like a firefly, we see others before we interact or talk. Sometimes people are more or less attractive, but most of the time those that make friends are sending out friendship signals. Friendship signals: eyebrow flash (quick up and down flash of the eyebrows when people meet), chin jut (foreword and up jut of the chin), etc. Eyebrow flashes include some short eye contact. This is not prolonged eye contact. The head tilt: exposing your carotid arteries because someone is not seen as a threat. Tilting to the right or left. Peoppenwho feel threatened tuck their chin into their chest to protect these arteries. The smile: portrays happiness, acceptance, liking, releases endorphins, etc., makes people feel good about themselves. There is a real smile and a fake smile. Make your smile genuine. Eye contact: no more than 1 second. Prolonged eye contact is perceived as predatory or creepy. End your eye contact with a smile. Eye contact is invading someone’s space, so only enter that space if continued invitation is given. Pupil dilation: wider dilation means we like people. Reciprocity: when people are given something they feel an obligation to give something back.  Touch: carefully. A gentle touch on the arm can go a long way. If rejected, people will pull away, etc. Hand touching is seen as more romantic. Pulling away means you need to build more repoire. Interlocked fingers is the most intimate of hand holding. Watch for nonverbal signals of rejection or acceptance when you make accidental physical touch. Isopraxism: mirroring. Mirror their body language. The mind does not notice mirrored body language, but it does notice when it is not mirrored. Something will feel off and someone will feel they are being sent a foe signal. Foreword lean: we lean towards people we like. We face people we like, etc. Whispering occurs between people who are close. Food forking: eating food off another’s plate seen as friendship, between those who know each other. Expressive gestures. Head-nodding: balanced frequency, without taking the spotlight. Verbal nudges: I see, go on, mm-hm (telling the speaker to continue). Focused attention (don’t let your phone interrupt real people interaction).  High tips: restate their order so they feel you are like them, do favors for reciprocity, introduce yourself by name, look good. Invading personal space with eyes is seens as offensive. A body scan will be invading. Eye rolls tell others you think something is stupid. Furrowed brows (can be misinterpreted). Aggressive stance (wide stance, lowering center of gravity). Watch for eye rolls, etc. That mean they doubt your message’s validity. Preach to these people, not the choir. Clothing and accessories: your appearance can be a foe or friend signal based on context. Birds of a feather flock together, in appearance especially when meeting people. People who engage in perception management often overlook the details, only buying nice shoes but not shining them, or wearing nice suits but using a cheap wallet. Masks are a foe signal, all kinds. We all have territory, or personal space, physical boundaries. Those who send friend signals invite us into more of their personal space. Stop your foreword progress when people back away or send foe signals. Mental health, trauma, culture, etc., can influence territorial boundaries. Dogs and humans perceive staring as a threatening foe gesture. Before you start talking, let your feet do the talking. How do you break in, or when do you break in and start a conversation.  If people have feet askew or open, they signal they are willing to add people to their group, or welcome others to the conversation. If people face their feet toward each other, they are signaling they are in a closed, private conversation. Do not rudely interrupt, wait for a natural pause in the conversation. Referencing older conversations signals you are not a newcomer. Don’t bask in the spotlight, but intentionally send friendship signals. The spotlight effect: when you do things unbelievably and expect others to notice. Much like lying, people become suspicious. When you suspect you will be awkward, you are more likely to force the behavior, which makes it more suspicious. To err is human, and makes the human more likeable too. People like humans who are like them in being fallible. Romantic relationships often exhibit: handholding, move centerpiece, exchange eyebrow flashes, long eye contact, gestures, share food, smile, tilt heads, lean toward each other, whisper or lower voices, mirror posture. Broken relationships will lack these , and one-way relationships will show these in one direction. Practice people watching, to observe behavior in communication. Become genuinely interested in others. Signals set the stage for interaction, the groundwork for real connection and relationship. The golden rule of friendship: if you want people to like you, make them feel good about themselves. Focus your attention on the person you are befriending. If they feel good everything they encounter you, they will seek you out more. Forego your ego and focus on the others’ needs. Other people will be eager to fulfill your wants and needs if they like you. Focusing on the other is difficult because we are naturally egocentric. Contract empathic statements, such as “so you…” or reaffirming what they have said. Restate what they said to see if you got it right, but don’t repeat it word for word. A fine line separates flattery from compliments. Flattery is insincere and manipulative. As a relationship grows, compliments play an increasingly important role. They signal a continued interest and awareness of someone. Provide the opportunity for people to compliment themselves. They rarely have this chance. You can make connections through third party compliments, letting you gain friendship through social influence. Words create filters through which people see the world. Our preconceptions or primacy tint our view of the world, by coloring what we see in a specific light. When we are told someone is friendly, we will believe it and give them multiple chances to exhibit this behavior if it doesn’t appear immediately. In a land of foes, practice increasing proximity, frequency, duration, and intensity. Offer a gift, etc., give them proof you are not threatening. Similarity and likeness: we like people who are like us, with commonalities, etc. Finding commonalities: contemporaneous interests (i see you’re wearing a Cubs shirt, are you also a fan), empathic statements, vicarious experiences (my father was a baker), collateral benefit (being with someone when they feel good things) (try creating good experiences, exercise with other people, etc., frightening experiences, shared hardship), law of curiosity (behave in a manner that engages curiosity in others, a curiosity hook: spark curiosity), law of reciprocity (favor or gift, smiling makes others feel like smiling back), disclosures (reciprocity in vulnerable disclosure. Done too early and you appear insecure. Self disclosure is often reciprocal), breadcrumb approach (disclosure over a long period of time, instead of opening the flood gates), law of personal attractiveness (anyone can increase their attractiveness with character and how they interact with others. Attractive people get and achieve more), law of humor (lightheartedness can increase trust, by releasing endorphins). The more we meet and interact with people, the more we are likely to become friends. The law of familiarity (more contact increases relationship), law of association (we see and perceive people by their company), law of self-esteem (self-esteem means you care kess what others do in response to your self disclosure). Increased restraint increases drive. If you tell your child they can’t do something, they want to do it all the more.  The more you can be interested, invite the other to communicate and respond empathically, the more people will feel good about themselves when with you. Share the glory with others, help them feel like they contributed, even if you did it all.   Active listening :LOVE. Listen, Observe, Vocalize, Empathize. Use empathic statements, don’t interrupt.   Lip perse: signals dissention or disagreement. Pay attention to nonverbal cues. Word mines: words that have different connotations to others and offend them. Lip bite: signals hesitation or holding back, that you want to say something. Lip  compression together: indicates something to say, but reluctance to do so. Lip touching externally: indicates unease, sensitivity, discomfort. Vocalize: how you say something is as important or more than the words. Tone of voice can convey what words cannot. Speed also regulates conversation, dragging out word can emphasize,. Intonation: lowering voice at end of sentence signals you are finished speaking.  Pause before speaking to give introverts time to think before speaking. When you are right, give someone a face-saving way to extricate themselves from the situation:  Ask for advice to help them feel good about themselves, give them a choice or illusion of control so they feel autonomous, elevate their status by relating them to important people (this painting reminds me of monet, this writing reminds me of Jane Austin). use illicitation (don’t just ask for direct information), ask presumptive questions so they feel they must correct your inaccuracy, empathic illicitation (empathic statements, that are presumptive, will be corrected). Use the law of reciprocity to get people to share information. Third-party illicitation: people will talk more readily about others than themselves. Ask them about someone else who uses drugs, is fat, does such and such job, etc. Common conversation pitfalls: talking about negative topics, talking about your own problems or world problems, talking about yourself only, meaningless chatter, extreme or too little emotion.  Building closness: repoir is what you have when you are close with someone. Test for repoir, especially when we want to have a meaningful relationship over time. Eye gaze is a measure of repoir. Touching is a Guage of the level of relationship. A women’s touch simply means she likes you, nothing more. The small of her back is one of the least sexual places, reserved for only the men she feels truly comfortable with. Other places touched mean the relationship has likely progressed beyond friendship or such. Preening or grooming: self preening especially to avoid interacting indicates dislike of someone. Grooming or preening a partner indicates a relationship. For example: fingers through their hair, shaving, removing dust, etc., swatting bugs or such, wipe food or crumbs off person’ps body. Mirroring: test repoir by changing body position and seeing if they mirror. Hair flip, with mutual gaze, is indicative of a positive relationship, while no eye gaze indicates bad repoir. Leaning together. Open posture. Fully engaged people will face you.  Crossing arms, building barriers with body or objects, or closed posture are a foe signal.  Removing barriers suggests increasing repoir, such as removing cups or pillows between people. friendship signals: eyebrow flashes, head tilt, smiling, inward lean, mirroring, expressive gestures, listening, removing barriers, puckering/licking lips, hair flip, grooming. Foe signals: furrowed brows, cold stairs, eye rolls, prolonged closed eyes or gaze aversion, no touching, leaning away, asynchronous posture, hair twirling (unless a habit), aggressive stance, creating barriers, fake yawns, self preening, scrunched nose. All long-term relationships start out as short-te relationships. In crisis, true character is shown. Do we truly care? Simple acts of kindness have a profound impact. Kind words have a long half-life. Active listening allows two-way open communication. Intimacy can be used for both care and harm, so resist the temptation to use sensitive information in troubled times or explode or outburst. Compliment your partner when they make a good point, help them save face, etc. Negativistic  partners call attention to mistakes and ignore successes. Be sure to acknowledge good things and positive qualities, as no one likes to have their virtues ignored and their flaws pestered and illuminated. Don't set unrealistic standards for yourself or your partner, be sure to temper your expectations for your partner. Sadistic partners weigh good with bad, replacing all the good with one mistake. Give your partner simple praise, notice their small successes. Praise doesn't cost money, only attention. Give public arecognition to your partner, modestly but generously. Ask your partner what they want, and listen to what they say. Anger management: attention on the angry party, letting them vent. Do not engage angry people as they are not thinking clearly, feeling threatened. Let them cool down. Offer a solution (explanation)  to help them feel more understanding and control (since anger is a lack of control symptom).  Big three approach: empathic statements, venting, presumptive statements. Break the anger cycle by empathizing effectively. Empathic statements do not feel patronizing usually because: angry people cannot reason well, and they expect people to listen to them and be sympathetic. The key is to identify the root cause for their anger, rather than state the obvious. “You feel overwhelmed because I haven't been home to help with the kids. You value the time you spend with friends because it gives you a break”. Once people vent their frustrations, they can increaseopenness to solutions.angry people have trouble not following along with presumptive statements “why don't I take the kids to mom's and we can go to a restaurant, you deserve it”. If they reject your suggested course of action, go through the process again.  Help them vent more. Give them two options to allow the illusion of choice. Airline interaction: the introverted strength. The internet gives introverts a fighting chance with time to formulate and think, pre-qualify potential friends, etc. It also offers the risk of immortality. The price is a cyber footprint. This is nice for you to see what others are like, but not nice for you if you make mistakes online. Learn digital etiquette. Keep your digital footprint small. In a digital world, it is hard to remain anonymous. You can assume that anything you post online is public. Detecting deception (especially online): well… (if you ask a yes or no question, and they begin with well…, it is likely they are going to give an answer you don'texpect, or stretch the truth in some way), land of “is” (argue over definition, “avoiding the question”), ask “why should I believe you?” (Lyers tend to bolster their credibility rather than reiterating that they are telling the truth). Be honest in your online portray, because deception starts the relationship out with betrayal. Internet communication removes the cues we use to assess people. Truth bias: people tend to trust others and what they say, until proven wrong. Be aware of this when you receive online communications, be skeptical. Primacy effect alters our perception of reality.offer competing hypotheses to yourself, and seek evidence for each of your hypotheses , even the first primary bias. Contrast plays a role in attraction, so you will compare a lone internet communicator with your ideal person, and you will over time attribute qualities of your ideal to them. Online, we tend to increase self-disclosure, which increases chances for catfishing and such. Cognitive dissonance (we assume we are reasonable and intelligent, and it is uncomfortable to accept the reality that we have been the victim of catfishing, etc. Sunk cost fallacy). Cognitive dissonance tends to make people disbelieve their misfortune. Expected milestones in communication are often missing online. Insist on a face-to-face meeting as early as possible, to prove they are not catfish, and that they are who they say they are. Choose a simple activity, like a short chat at a coffee shop.
