Notes: Be authentic, don’t fake it. Emotions are contagious, so step one to being liked and helping others feel great is to only go to events that make you happy, so you don't have to fake it. Create your own rules… 1. play to your position (what are you good at? What fits you? Don’t play center if you’re not tall enough): Places to socialize, your thrive locations: restaurants, phone calls, video chat, nature, backyard BBQ, dinner parties, movie night. Your survive locations are places you dislike. Think of your 3 favorite locations, your thrive locations. Accept invitations or plan events in your thrive locations. 2. Work the room: don’t hover at the start zone, where people are not yet comfortable or willing to open up. The side zone: bathroom, food, people you know. These are traps. The best thing to do is to wave and say hi to friends and tell them you’ll catch them later. Capitalize on your fresh energy. The social zone: “where people exit the bar”, where they are ready to mingle, where the host can introduce you, the couch or bartable. Be a grader, take breaks between “courses” in the meal. Go for the appetizer, main course, dessert, etc. 3. Know your team: who do you love spending time with? Who makes you laugh? Who do you most look forward to seeing? Who do you wish you could get to know better? Who do you go to for strategizing? Who makes you feel valued? Who do you call in a crisis? Who makes you feel like your best self? Your winger: helps you feel comfortable and shares your goals for growth. Your riser: someone you wish was on this list. Rely on the people that help you thrive. You do not have to like everyone. Say no, you will remember who you are. Take control, play to your strengths. Challenge: say no to an event you are dreading. At your next event, find two sweet spots, identify someone as your riser. Reach out to a winger and invite them to socialize. Don’t socialize in ways that drain you. How to make a killer first-impression: people make first impressions in 2 seconds. This is to your advantage. Use your hands, posture, and eyes. Hands out of pockets. Keep hands visible. Pockets are murderers of rapoir. Shake their hand like a testing a peach. Stop squeezing, when you feel their muscles. Releasing Oxytocin helps people decide you are a friend, not foe. When we are proud, we want people to notice us, so we take up space. Use the launch stance. We sometimes move into loser stance by checking our phone. Avoid checking your phone before important meetings, etc. Do something else. Stand like a winner. Engage with eye contact, showing a desire for alliance. Experience along with others. Talking with or to, not at. Hold eye contact 60% of the time, to create trust, use gaze to produce connection. We look at people we like. If someone looks at another, the other will start to like the first. Holding eye contact calls on courage, and produces Oxytocin. When you like someone you look at them more. In most cultures, gaze helps others begin to like us. In the first few seconds, avoid the temptation to look away shyly. Big talk: skirting norms,  challenging chitchat status quo. Emotional peaks, or sparks. Use unique talking points to create pleasurable and memorable conversations.
