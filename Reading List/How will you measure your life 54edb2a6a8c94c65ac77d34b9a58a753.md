Notes: Theories help us know how to think about things we have not done yet. We can learn without experiencing the pain of every failure. Great lives are a byproduct of happiness and meaning. We don’t find satisfaction and meaning from getting a “good life”. What brings money, does not bring happiness. Hygiene factors (money, status, compensation, job security, etc.) are a byproduct of being happy with your job. Emergent vs deliberate strategy. When you find out what really works for you, then you use a deliberate strategy. Until then, use an emergent strategy, a strategy that emerges as you go along. A modified strategy coalescing from a myriad of day-to-day decisions, to pursue unanticipated opportunities, solving unanticipated problems. Unpredictable results bring unpredictable strategies. How can you decide what has the best choice of working for you? How to decide if you should go with a deliberate strategy or an emergent one, if you should make a big investment of time, etc.? Acknowledge your predictions are rough and inaccurate. Compile a list of assumptions made in these predictions. Rank order the list. Assess which assumptions must be true in order to expect these predictions to become true. Test the validity of the most important assumptions. What assumptions must prove true? Are these assumptions within your control? Are they based on extrinsic or extrinsic motivators? While you are still figuring it out, keep the aperture of your life open.  Continue to experiment until you do find an approach that works.  Expecting to have a clear vision just wastes time and may close off your mind to unexpected opportunities. Change is uncomfortable, being open and experimenting is uncomfortable. Watch where your resources flow.  Where you spend your resources is what you are executing, whether you planned a strategy or not.  Compensation sometimes incentivises expending resources in the wrong areas. Resource allocation is where the rubber meets the road. Vision, plans, and opportunities are all just that until resources are allocated, and they all vy for attention. Look at what you do, not what you say you will do. Pull out the check stub, or account purchase history. High achieving people often direct resources to the activities that yield the most tangible immediate accomplishments/consequences.  Short-term rewards take their resources. Many ambitious people allocate fewer and fewer resources to the things that they say matter most. With every decision about how you allocate your time and money , you are making a statement about what really matters to you. Does your family come out on top? We are tempted to invest energy un whatever activity yields immediate evidence that we have achieved something.  Set boundaries in your life, to keep yourself true to what you most value. The longterm payoff will be much more profound.  The time to invest in meaningful relationships is when it seems it is not necessary.  
Most businesses have to pivot, and yhe successful companies have enough leftover capital to invest in the new strategy after failing. Once a viable strategy has been found, pivot forward.  When we neglect our values, we end up seeking returns later on investments we didn’t make.  Investments in life cannot be sequenced.  By the time we move on to another investment, the game is already over. You have to “spin all the plates”. Face to face conversation and being naturally chatty with a child is important for children. It has a profound effect on children, most importantly in the first year of life . The effect is exponential. Children who have been exposed to extra talk have incalculable advantage. A tiny investment has a huge return. You can’t miss this window. The investment is made long before it seems to pay off. Invest long before you need the fruit.  What job did you hire that milkshake for?  We hire or buy products to do jobs for us. Students hire school to feel accomplished and have something to do. Schools often do a different job, instead of offering students success everyday. Many unhappy marriages are built on “selflessness”  based upon what we want. We give what we think others need. We project what we want, and forget to ask what people really need, what jobs they truly need done. Don’t make a chocolatier milkshake, do the job to be done. The path to happiness is about finding someone who you want to make happy.   We feel attached to things because we have given so much of ourselves to them. Sacrifice deepens our commitment. It’s important that what we sacrifice for is worthy of that commitment.  Thinking of what jobs need to be done allows you to develop true empathy. Then do the job. Don’t outsource the future.  Figure out what capabilities you’ll need to succeed in the future, and keep those in-house.  As parents, we can help our children develop capabilities. They have resources, processes, and priorities. Resources = what you use, processes = how you do it, priorities = why you do it. Many parents are removing the resources by which kids develop processes. They are creating the Greek tragedy of Dell, outsourcing their capabilities and unintentionally inhibiting their children’s growth and development of processes.  Providing opportunities for our kids should serve to solve their jobs, not ours. Don’t outsource your children’s resources and activities without being sure they are productive and that your kids have learned to learn, to think, develop new knowledge, processes, etc. Start early to help children solve problems on their own. Help them build confidence in their capabilities, that they can solve their own problems. They will feel pride that they have done it. Children learn when they are ready to learn. When we outsource, we often outsource our values. They learn when they are ready to learn, and not when we are ready to teach.   If your children get their values and beliefs and priorities from other people, who's children are they? Have you truly patented them? Don’t outsource your role as a parent. If you’re not with your children in their challenges, you are missing an opportunity to shape their priorities and lives. What lessons are you letting your kids have? Those with inadequate experience have taken the wrong courses. Often, enforcing the rules is harder on parents than children.  Help children see success in things you want to be part of your culture.  Your solution to problems will become expected. How you react to events comes to become boreal for your children. They will see what you do as expected, it will become your family culture. If you don’t consciously form culture, it will form, perhaps in a way you don't like. If you  let your children get away with lazy or disrespectful culture a few times will allow it to become culture. So will telling them you are proud of them when they accomplish something. Live a life of integrity. Full vs marginal thinking. Never give in to “just this once”.  Ignore sunk costs. It’s tempting to think that you can leverage capital already gained to tackle new challenges, to incur a marginal cost, but you will end up paying the full cost anyway. 100% of the time is easier than 98% of the time. Don’t do it “just this once” for the “marginal cost.” life is one undending stream of extenuating circumstances.  It’s easier to hold to your principles 100% of the time than it is to hold to them 98% of the time. Your personal moral line is powerful because you don’t cross it.  When you justify once, there’s nothing to stop you doing it again.  Decide what you stand for, and then stand for it all the time.  Never start making moral concessions in your life.  Whether they want one or not, every company has a purpose. Some purposes are simply given by emergent strategies, but if a company has chosen a clearance compelling purpose, it's impact and legacy can be extraordinary. The purpose serves as a beacon.  The 3 parts of purpose: likeness: what you want something to become at the end of the path you are on. Commitment: to the likeness. Metrics: to measure your progress toward the likeness.  If you find that your likeness isn’t who you want to become, then revisit your likeness and decide who you truly want to be.  Constrained by our mind, we cannot always see the big picture. We need to aggregate to start to see it.  God doesn’t need to aggregate. His measure of achievement is the individual. The only metrics we need are the individuals we help in whatever assignment or role we may have.  The more i focused on my problems, the more I despaired. Take the time to figure out your life purpose. Clarity about purpose will trump other concerns in the long-run.
