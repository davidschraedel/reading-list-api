Notes: Reminded me of the mystery of ancient life, meeting other tribes, nature known and unknown, and the Superstition inherent in ignorance. Also reminded me of Dr. Stone, where science is like sorcery. It is scary to those who don’t understand it, and is different than the inherent power the gods or humanity. It must be learned by experiment, and seems to be governed by other laws. I like the end where she seems to rid herself of her inhibitions and regrets by living out her desires and trying to put things right. The maiden mother matriarch archetype, the triple goddess. She is wildly and naively infatuated, she bares children, and then learns to leave it all. All with a feminist twist of seeing the pain and irrationality of it all, showing bad practical examples rather than good of her experience, offering more of the positive only in her reflection on what has transpired or her acceptance of it or aging.
