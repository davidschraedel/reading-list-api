Notes: We are naturally mindful in many situations that are novel or exciting (a date with someone we love, a beautiful view in nature). We get bored because we are not mindful, we are hooked by or fused with feelings or judgements that what is happening is bad or subpar in some way. Learning to experience life with more mindfulness and unhook from subjective judgements about our reality in each moment can help us see life for the beauty that it is. We get tired of things because we start to feel that we know them and there is nothing to pay attention to (we know the story the protocol, we know what will happen, we don’t need to notice anymore). Contrary to this assumption, things are always changing in our life, and we simply don’t notice. Savor every moment. One could say that to truly live as if you only live once means that you not only seek out pleasure, but enjoy the mundane and even attention to and notice the pain and discomfort because you won’t get it ever again. Discomfort often points to something important: something you need to do, something you care about, etc. We ruminate worry and obsess because it gives us temporary rewards. For example, escape, feeling like we are working hard, avoiding taking action. These payoffs or rewards are called reinforcing consequences. Our internal world is like a documentary. It misses 99% of what we have done in our life, and is often biased, yet it paints a specific picture and insists that this is who we are. It is The Big Story or our self-concept. Choose goals that are live person goals, not dead person goals, things that a live person can do better than a dead person. Instead of saying “i’ll stop smoking”, say “instead of smoking, i’ll run around the block, etc. Focus on what will replace what you want to change. Focus on starting things that make life better, a values-based life, rather than stopping things, a rules-based life. You will accomplish the rules along the way, or find out they were flawed anyway. Values are like what we bring to life’s table. We can’t choose what dishes are served, they change all the time, some are enjoyable for us, some are not. We can choose, though, to bring something to the table. Values can guide us in what we bring. Values are ways that we live in the present, the way we act toward the world, the way we become. Goals  are things to obtain. Goals are not mindful practices, they are the ends of practices that may be mindful or not. Our poor behavior doesn’t reflect our values, it means we are hooked by thoughts and feelings that pull us away from our values.  Values are desired qualities of behavior, how we want to behave, because we can choose. If there is something you aspire to be, by definition, that way of being is already a value you have. The only way to know if it’s really yours, if you really like it, if it’s really a benefit to you, is by trying it out, by tasting the food. You can smell and think all you want, philosophize all you want, but the knowing only comes by doing/trying. Take one bite, think small, take baby steps. Small steps over time have large effects. Choose a value or two each morning that you will bring into play that day.  As you try it, notice what it is like, see what you feel and think about it, if it’s doing what you want for you. Make small changes in one area of your life, like a sculptor. You can’t focus on them all at the same time. You have to chip in one spot and try to make that what you want before moving to another spot. You always have two choices for making things better: leave the situation, or stick with it and do what you can to influence it for the better and ask for help and treat yourself kindly. You can’t always leave, but you can always do your part to make something better.  Domains of life: work, relationships, play/recreation, health. All domains overlap. So small changes in one create a domino effect. You can ripple positive change into your life. Find values in each domain. Some may appear in multiple domains. Now that you have domains and values, pick one domain for the week, and one short-term goal based on those values, that is not too easy or hard, not not emotional or based on how to feel, and not a dead person goals. Make it specific, realistic, and anticipate that it will be hard. We always can choose our attitude, our way in life. We only have the present. The past is memory in the present, the future images and thought in the present, but the present is experience. Savor it and make the most of it.
