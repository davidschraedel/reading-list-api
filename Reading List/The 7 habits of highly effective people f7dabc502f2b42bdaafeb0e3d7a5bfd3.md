Notes: Character ethics, not personality ethics. There are laws that cannot be broken, only fought against. Personality ethics is trying to take a short-cut. But can you really shortcut life, like skipping years of piano or sports practice? We have paradigms or ways that we see the world, maps of the territory. A map ≠ territory, it just represents or interprets parts of it. How we see the world impacts what we do. We are conditioned to see in specific ways, and thus have differing perspectives when seeing the same facts. By changing our paradigms, living by the principles of life - or valuing true principles, we can change much quicker than otherwise, and truly change instead of masking growth in a world of similar fakes. Who we are shines brighter than how we communicate, etc. We cannot escape showing our true character, thus why we trust some people even with their clumsy skills in speaking, etc. Life is, by nature, highly interdependent. Those who can’t choose interdependence well often are fleeing from internal dependencies, such as letting others’ weaknesses ruin our emotional lives, or feeling victimized by things out of our control. Thus people abandon marriages, children, and many other social responsibilities in the name of “liberation”, “living their truth”, “asserting themselves”, “authenticity”.  Interdependence is a far more mature advanced life, where you are self-reliant and capable, but also realize that far more is accomplished when we work together.  You derive a great sense of worth from within, but also recognize the need for love, giving, and receiving love. You realize you need the best thinking of others to join your own. Being interdependent, youcan share yourself deeply and meaningfully, and have access to others’ potential and resources. Interdependence is a choice only independent people can make. Dependent people don’t own enough of themselves. Private victories precede public victories. Habits 1, 2, and 3 move a person from dependence to independence. 4, 5, and 6 are built upon this base, to work on public victories in the world of interdependence. Effectiveness is both the golden eggs and the goose that laid them. Effectiveness = production, and production capability. We look for shortcut returns, we often lose our production capacity. We stop taking care of the lawn mower, or spend the money that earns us interest, or stop developing ourselves. We must continually improve our own production capacity, to avoid being dependent or locked into situations. When we focus on outcomes rather than preserving the underlying relationship, we can become impatient, neglectful, controlling, logically faulty and justifying. The love, richness, spontaneity, and commitment of real relationships is a product of our prior investment in the production capacity, the relationship. Don’t kill the goose, or poison or let it become malnourished.  Don’t leave others in an organization with “dying geese” physical assets that you didn’t upkeep. many times, others are our production capacity for the things we desire, because only together can we accomplish the goal.  You can buy a person’s hand or back, but you can’t truly buy their heart or brain. You can buy agreements and labor, but not relationship and ingenuity. Employees, customers, family, friends, are all volunteers offering their best parts: their heart and mind, and they can choose to withdraw or offer them somewhere else. So much focus on the golden egg kills the goose. That short-term bottom line isn’t all-importan0t. Effectiveness lies in balance. Focusing only on PC or the goose is like spending a life doing nothing but running only to brag about running, or being an eternal student that never produces any eggs. Read as if, in the next 48 hours, you will teach someone what you read. As you live these principles, you will care less about what others think of you, and more about what others think of themselves and their worlds, including their relationship with you. Your emotional life will no longer be built on others’ weaknesses. “That which we obtain too easily, we esteem too lightly…heaven knows how to put a proper price on its goods”. Between the stimulus and response, man has the freedom to choose. As humans, we have imagination, conscience, and independent will. We are self-aware. Like Victor frankl, we can choose how we respond even when all other choices are taken away. We are not determined. We study animals and ill people to decipher human behavior, but these do not represent the territory of man’s  experience/capability. If we do not account for how we see ourselves and others, our awareness, we are unable to understand how others perceive and feel about themselves and the world, and we project ourselves on others while thinking we are objective reality. Our behavior is a function of our decisions, not our conditions. Response-ability, the ability to choose your response = responsibility, not reactivity.  Responsible people are value driven, they choose based on their values.  To be reactive is to empower others’ weaknesses to control your feelings, thoughts, behaviors.  Until you say “I am what I am today because of the choices I made yesterday”, you cannot say “I choose otherwise”. It’s not what happens, it’s our response. Our basic nature is to act, not be acted upon. Good habits depend upon your proactive muscles. Use your resourcefulness and initiative. Many people wait for others to help them. Create an atmosphere where people can seize opportunities and solve problems in an increasingly self-reliant way. If you wait, you will be acted upon.  Act, or be acted upon. To be proactive is to face reality, and then choose to respond for the better. It’s not positive thinking, it’s choosing positive responses.  We can see how we think about our power by our language. Do we use reactive or proactive language? We have a circle of influence and a circle of concern. Focusing on the circle of influence makes it grow, and vice versa.  For everything, we either have direct control, indirect control, or no control.  We solve direct control problems by working on habits. We solve indirect control problems by changing our methods of influence. We solve “no control” problems by learning to peacefully accept and live with them.  These three proactive approaches are all within and expand our circle of influence. Focus on what you choose to be, not what you have or do not have. Like Joseph in Egypt, you may be unjustly imprisoned, but you can always choose what you want to be and how to use your influence. Choose your own weather, by accepting the things you can’t control, and focusing effort on things you can.  We choose actions, not consequences. Picking up a stick with consequences you don’t want is a past mistake, and is in your circle of concern, not influence. acknowledge and learn from the mistake immediately. It is not others or our mistakes that hurt us, but our response to them. As we make and keep commitments, we establish an inner integrity, awareness of self-control, and courage and strength to accept more responsibility. Little by little, our honor becomes greater than our mood, because we keep our promises to ourselves.
